issues.txt  --  the consolidated issue list, three analyses merged
============================================================================

WHAT THIS IS. Three independent analyses of rules.txt were run -- a rule
inventory of all 249 paragraphs, an annex of 49 unchecked behaviours, and a
log of 16 open design questions -- plus a first formal grammar. They overlap
heavily and nobody merged them. This file is the merge. Every issue has one
id, every source that found it is named, and every citation in it was
re-checked against rules.txt, glossary.txt, grammar.ebnf and the corpus.

A FIFTH SOURCE WAS ADDED LATER. A parser was built from grammar.ebnf and run
on all three corpus programs. It is the only input to this file that is an
execution rather than a reading, and it produced Z-41 through Z-46. Its
citations into grammar.ebnf are against the repaired grammar; every earlier
grammar.ebnf citation in this file was re-checked against the same file and
corrected where it had drifted.

THE CORPUS IS THE ARBITER. unified.z, json.z and ecs.z are the only evidence
about this language that is not an opinion. Where an analysis and the corpus
disagreed, the corpus won and the finding was downgraded, corrected or
killed. What was killed is listed at the end, under CLAIMS I COULD NOT
CONFIRM.

SOURCE ABBREVIATIONS
    INV     the rule inventory (an internal working document, not
            shipped in this tree), findings A-E
    ANX     the unchecked-behaviour annex (unchecked.txt), ids U.*
    OQ      the open-questions log (open.txt), ids OQ-0..OQ-15
    GRM     grammar.ebnf, where its header records an invented resolution
    PRS     the parser built from grammar.ebnf and run on the corpus,
            findings Z-41 .. Z-46
    ADJ     this consolidation, from the corpus, where no input had it

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE BANDS

    BLOCKING   The language cannot be specified until this is decided. A
               parser cannot be written, or two rules contradict such that
               no implementation could satisfy both.

    SOUNDNESS  A rule as written admits programs that are wrong, or the
               spec commits to something the corpus already violates.

    GAP        Used in the corpus, specified nowhere. Mechanical to fix
               once someone writes it down.

    DEFECT     The document contradicts itself, cites something that is not
               there, or states one rule in several places with drift.

    CORPUS     A bug in the .z programs, not in the language.

Within a band, issues are ordered by how much else they block.

Z-04 and Z-28 were closed together by giving holes an explicit marker,
`name :? T`. Both rested on a hole being inferred from a missing binder;
neither was resolved by choosing between its readings. Counts above are
kept at their original values with the closures noted, because an id
belongs to the thing and a retired entry is kept and marked.

Z-15, Z-16 and Z-20 were closed together and in that order, because each
needed the one before it: `slice` could not be declared until there was a
scope to declare it in, and the predeclared list could not be written until
`tag_of` had a definition to be listed with. All three were GAPs, which is
to say the corpus had already decided them and nobody had written it down.
None required a design decision, and none changed a spelling in the corpus.
The one place a choice was made -- `Token.tag.colon()` against [P19]'s
parenless `Token.tag.colon` -- went to the existing rule; it is recorded at
sum.tag.ctor.1.

45 findings from the three inputs were folded into 15 of these issues as
duplicates. 6 findings were rejected or corrected; they are at the bottom.

The last six, Z-41 through Z-46, came later and from the parser rather than
from the three inputs. Ids are positional in this file only by accident:
they belong to the thing and not to the band, which is why the bands are no
longer contiguous runs. One of the six, Z-44, is recorded as FIXED AND
VERIFIED rather than open.

THE FIVE THAT MATTER MOST

  1. Z-01  Nothing inside a function body is specified. rules.txt names
           `if else while for loop return continue break` once, in a
           keyword budget (rules.txt:352), and gives no form and no
           semantics for any of them, while the corpus contains 28
           three-clause `for` loops, 6 `while`, 4 `loop` and 4 `continue`.
           grammar.ebnf had to invent the whole statement grammar, the
           semicolon rule and the precedence table. This is the largest
           single volume of missing specification in the project.

  2. Z-02  `[]` means five different things and no rule picks between them.
           Two normative rules -- "`p[i]` on a raw pointer is the element"
           and "`p[i]` is `p.[](i)`" -- are mutually exclusive on the same
           expression, both spellings occur inside `List` itself, and a
           fifth case (indexing a `slice`) is in the corpus and in no rule
           at all. A reader cannot determine what a program means.

  3. Z-06  Decide the one bit: does a pointee carry a qualifier, yes or no.
           It is the root of the design log, rules.txt itself calls it "the
           deepest hole in the design" (rules.txt:249) and then schedules
           something else, and Z-09 and Z-25 both wait on it. It is a
           yes/no and it is answerable this week.

  4. Z-07  Arithmetic has no overflow, division-by-zero or shift rule, and
           the standard library already depends on wrapping multiplication
           to compute the value it intends (json.z:106, ecs.z:128). This is
           a commitment already made in code and not yet made in prose. It
           has no dependencies and should be written before anything else
           acquires a dependency on it.

  5. Z-04  CLOSED. Was: BODILESS MEANS HOLE (rules.txt:132) against "a def
           whose body would be empty may drop it -- `Span :: (lo, hi) ->
           self` is a record" (rules.txt:80-81), with five bodiless records
           in the corpus proving the second. The criterion the document
           needed was the binder, which the first rule never mentioned.
           Holes are now marked `:?`, so nothing is inferred from an
           absence and the criterion is gone rather than chosen. See Z-04.

So four of the five now stand. No issue was promoted into the empty slot:
the candidate is discussed two paragraphs down and does not qualify.

Cheapest large win, taken: Z-20, the sum discriminant, closed along with
Z-15 and Z-16. It was fully specified already in ecs.z:1142-1148, had zero
dependencies, changed no existing spelling, and closed frictions in both
programs. It cost twenty rules in spec.txt across the three issues.

Next cheapest, on the same test -- decided by the corpus, no dependencies,
no spelling changed: Z-21, pointer arithmetic, which the corpus performs in
two places and no rule mentions; and Z-15's leftovers, which are now
[UNSPECIFIED] markers rather than an absent type. Neither is a large win.
The next LARGE win is not cheap and is Z-06.

Closest to displacing one of the five, and it does not: Z-41. It is Z-03's
proposed answer rather than a sixth independent hole, and Z-03 is not in the
five either. It is nevertheless the only issue in this file that says a fix
already written does not work, and anyone writing a parser should read it
next to Z-03 and Z-46.

Z-01  NO STATEMENT OR CONTROL-FLOW FORM IS SPECIFIED ANYWHERE   BLOCKING

WHAT      rules.txt lists eight control-flow keywords in a budget and never
          states the syntax or the semantics of any of them.

EVIDENCE  rules.txt:352  "    if else while for loop return continue break"
          -- the only occurrence of `while`, `loop`, `continue` or `break`
          in the file outside prose. glossary.txt:367 repeats the same line
          and adds only `defer  run at scope exit` (glossary.txt:366).
          The corpus: 28 three-clause `for` loops, e.g. unified.z:183
          "for (i := 0; i < self.count; i += 1) { new_items[i] = ... }";
          6 `while`; 4 `loop`, e.g. json.z:152-162; 4 `continue`, e.g.
          json.z:585; 0 `break` anywhere in any .z file.
          grammar.ebnf:353-382 invents Statement, WhileStmt, ForStmt,
          LoopStmt, DeferStmt, ReturnStmt, BreakStmt, ContinueStmt;
          grammar.ebnf:329 invents IfExpr; grammar.ebnf:137-165 invents a
          Go-style semicolon-insertion rule; grammar.ebnf:270-284 invents
          the precedence table, noting "The sources say only that
          `precedence is a fixed table`; this is that table."

FOUND BY  GRM, which had to invent all of it and says so at
          grammar.ebnf:25-27. ADJ, from the corpus counts. INV does not
          raise it because it inventories what rules.txt says, not what it
          omits; ANX raises only the two evaluation-order fragments (U.10.1
          `defer`, U.10.2 operand order); OQ raises only the missing
          traversal form (OQ-3). No input saw the whole hole.

BLOCKS    A parser. Every function body in the corpus. The semantics of
          `defer` (Z-23), of `return` inside a match arm, and of whether a
          `loop` value is an expression.

OPTIONS   grammar.ebnf:353-382 is already a complete C-style proposal, and
          grammar.ebnf:137-165 a complete termination rule, both derived
          from the corpus. Adopt or reject them explicitly; the grammar
          says its positions are "NOT argued here".

Z-02  `[]` HAS FIVE MEANINGS AND NO DISAMBIGUATION RULE         BLOCKING

WHAT      One syntax carries five incompatible readings and the predicate
          that chooses between them is stated nowhere.

EVIDENCE  rules.txt:46  "    []   indexing               xs[0]"
          rules.txt:159 List's accessor projects the element's ADDRESS;
            unified.z:198 "[] :: (&self, i: u32) -> ptr<T> { &self.items[i] }"
          rules.txt:198-200 Map projects a COPY;
            json.z:208  "[] :: (&self, k: K) -> Option<V> { self.get(k) }"
            used at json.z:680 -- `obj["tags"]` matched as an Option.
          rules.txt:193-194 "`p[i]` ON A RAW POINTER IS THE ELEMENT; `xs[i]`
            ON A LIST IS ITS ADDRESS. One syntax, two meanings, and both
            appear inside List itself";
            unified.z:183  "new_items[i] = self.items[i]"  (raw, no `.*`)
            unified.z:168  "self.items[self.count] = item"
          rules.txt:496 "a symbolic member -- `p[i]` is `p.[](i)` and
            reaches too";  ecs.z:365, ecs.z:507, unified.z:246 index
            through a pointer to a container.
          FIFTH CASE, in no rule and no glossary entry: indexing a slice.
            unified.z:277-278  "hi := digits[v / 16]" where
            `digits :: "0123456789abcdef"`; json.z:106 "k.ptr[i]".
          A SIXTH shape exists as a named member rather than `[]`:
            ecs.z:164  "at :: (&self, e: Entity) -> Option<ptr<C>>".
          rules.txt:193-196 itemises the collision and files it under a
          heading of PRICES, not as an unresolved rule.

FOUND BY  INV (B1, "most serious"), ANX twice and independently of itself
          (U.3.2 raw-vs-List, U.3.3 varies-across-containers, plus U.3.1
          for the aliasing consequence), and GRM, whose single production
          `Index = "[" Expression "]"` (grammar.ebnf:318) cannot express
          the difference. Two of the three analyses, three findings.
          NOT found by OQ, which touches `[]` only at OQ-3 as the reason
          `List` could close its projection -- see CLAIMS, item 6.

BLOCKS    The meaning of every container expression in the language. The
          memory model (which of these yields something you can alias). The
          `slice` type (Z-15). Z-14, since `Map`'s reading is the one that
          forces its storage open.

OPTIONS   INV proposes one rule with an explicit precedence, and guesses
          the predicate is "does the pointee declare a `[]` member" -- and
          states plainly that this predicate "appears nowhere" in the
          sources. Treat it as a guess, not as a candidate. Beyond that:
          no candidate resolution in the sources.

Z-03  `<` IS BOTH COMPARISON AND THE COMPILE-TIME BRACKET       BLOCKING

WHAT      The same token opens a compile-time argument list and compares
          two values, and nothing in any source says how a parser tells
          them apart.

EVIDENCE  rules.txt:43  "    <>   compile-time args      List<u8>, ptr<T>"
          rules.txt:456 lists `< <= > >=` as instructions on the primitives.
          grammar.ebnf:314 "Suffix = Selector | Deref | Index | Arguments
          | TypeArgs | BlockLit ." -- so any PrimaryExpr may be followed by
          `<`, while grammar.ebnf:292 "RelExpr = AddExpr { rel_op AddExpr }"
          also consumes `<`. The corpus puts both in one file and one
          function: ecs.z:364 "for (i := 0; i < self.stores.count; i += 1)"
          against ecs.z:519 "ptr<System<M>>".
          GRM sees half of the problem and fixes only that half:
          grammar.ebnf:101-103 "`>>` and `>>=` are single tokens. A parser
          closing TypeArgs splits a `>>` into two `>`... so that
          `ptr<List<T,_>>` lexes."

FOUND BY  GRM for the `>>` half only. ADJ for the `<` half, from the two
          corpus sites above. No input raised the opening bracket.

BLOCKS    A parser, independently of Z-01. Also the precedence table, since
          `<` sits at level 4 and level 8 at once in grammar.ebnf:270-284.

OPTIONS   No candidate resolution in the sources. (The `>>` half has one:
          grammar.ebnf:101-103.)

Z-04  BODILESS MEANS HOLE CONTRADICTS THE BODILESS RECORD       BLOCKING
      -- CLOSED. Holes are marked `:?`; bodiless is no longer a criterion.

CLOSED    A hole is now written `name :? T` and is never inferred from an
          absence, so "bodiless" classifies nothing and the two rules stop
          overlapping. This resolves the issue by removing the criterion it
          rested on rather than by choosing between the readings below --
          neither OPTION was taken. The five bodiless declarations in the
          corpus are records because they carry `::`, which is now the only
          thing that was ever distinguishing them.
          Landed in: grammar.ebnf Declaration and SignatureType,
          spec.txt hole.bodiless.1 (restated) and bind.marked.1 (new),
          glossary.txt HOLE and MEMBER. The corpus was converted: 42 holes
          across the three files.
          The entry is kept and marked rather than deleted, per README.

WHAT      Two rules classify the same text two ways, and the corpus is on
          one side.

EVIDENCE  rules.txt:132-136  "BODILESS MEANS HOLE, so a hand-written header
          full of `func insert_item<T>(...);` declares an ABSTRACT MODULE".
          rules.txt:80-81  "A def whose body would be empty may drop it --
          `Span :: (lo: u32, hi: u32) -> self` is a record declaration."
          Five bodiless declarations in the corpus are records, not holes:
            json.z:77   "Span :: (lo: u32, hi: u32) -> self"
            json.z:136  "    Entry :: (key: K, value: V) -> self"
            json.z:268  "Error :: (at: u32, why: slice<u8>) -> self"
            ecs.z:258   "Health :: (hp: i32, max: i32) -> self"
            ecs.z:413   "Hit :: <A, B> (e: Entity, a: ptr<A>,
                                       b: ptr<B>) -> self"
          The distinguishing property is the binder, which rules.txt:132
          never mentions even though rules.txt:48-49 has already made the
          binder the thing that carries meaning.
          Compounding it: the only illustration of the rule is written in
          another language -- `func` and a statement-terminating `;` exist
          nowhere in zlang and in no other line of rules.txt.

FOUND BY  INV (B7, second of its three most serious) and, independently,
          GRM, which resolved it by the binder without flagging the
          conflict: grammar.ebnf:217-220 makes a hole `MemberName ":" Type`
          with no binder, and grammar.ebnf:188 makes a bodiless signature
          the TYPE of a hole rather than a hole itself.

BLOCKS    The classification of every declaration in the language, and
          therefore the hole rules, the shape/policy split, and the type
          test at rules.txt:309-311.

OPTIONS   INV: restate as "a member with no binder is a hole, whether or
          not a body follows", and delete "bodiless" as the criterion.
          GRM has already implemented exactly that.

Z-05  `-> oneof {a, b}` IS SPECIFIED AND CANNOT BE PARSED       BLOCKING

WHAT      rules.txt gives a subset-sum projection whose spelling collides
          with a `-> oneof` block's body, and the grammar has no production
          for it.

EVIDENCE  rules.txt:128-130  "`-> oneof {a, b}` is a sum over a subset --
          variants only the declaring module can construct, matchable by
          everyone. It falls out of visibility below rather than being
          designed."
          grammar.ebnf:247-249  "Projection = "oneof" | ProjectionList
          | Type ." -- `oneof` and a list are alternatives, never a
          sequence. The form has no parse.
          The collision is not hypothetical: a `-> oneof` block's body is
          also braces, json.z:138-142
              Slot :: -> oneof {
                  empty: ()
                  dead:  ()
                  live:  Entry
              }
          so `-> oneof {a, b}` reads as a `oneof` followed by a body
          declaring `a` and `b` -- which is not a valid body either, since
          grammar.ebnf:217-218 requires a Declaration to carry a `:` and a
          type, or a binder.
          Zero uses in the corpus.
          "Falls out of visibility below" points forward to rules.txt:147,
          and the derivation is never shown.

FOUND BY  INV (C3: zero corpus support, derivation not shown, and the
          forward reference logged separately as E5) and GRM (no
          production). Two sources, describing it differently: INV read it
          as an unexercised rule, GRM as an unparseable one. Both are true
          and the second is the one that blocks.

BLOCKS    Whether `-> oneof` takes a body, a projection list, or both.
          How a partially-projected sum interacts with the exhaustiveness
          rule at rules.txt:368-369, which nothing states.

OPTIONS   No candidate resolution in the sources.

Z-41  THE `<` DISAMBIGUATION RULE IS UNVALIDATED: THE CORPUS
      CANNOT TELL IT FROM FIVE WEAKER ONES                      BLOCKING

WHAT      Z-03 says nothing decides between `<` the comparison and `<` the
          compile-time bracket. A rule has since been written and a parser
          built from it. The parser makes the right decision on every `<`
          in the corpus -- and so do five strictly weaker rules, the
          weakest being bracket balance with no lookahead at all. The
          corpus therefore does not validate the rule. It cannot tell the
          rule apart from guessing.

EVIDENCE  PRS. On the three corpus programs the rule made 166 decisions and
          got 166 right. Five progressively weaker rules were then run
          against the same 166 inputs and every one of them also scored
          166 out of 166, bracket balance alone included. The two clauses
          the grammar actually names as the discriminator never once
          discriminated: on no corpus input did the decision turn on
          either of them.
          The hard case is not decided by the rule, it is declared.
          grammar.ebnf:432-446 states the position: where a token scan
          cannot tell the two apart, THE ARGUMENT-LIST READING WINS, so in
          `if (a < b && c > (d))` the `<` opens a compile-time argument
          list applied to `b && c`, and the escape is to parenthesise the
          comparison -- `if ((a < b) && (c > (d)))`. The grammar says as
          much in the same place: "This is a genuine ambiguity, not an
          oversight; the rule above resolves it by fiat and the
          parenthesised spelling is the escape." That is a decision
          recorded, not a problem solved, and no source document
          authorises it. No input in the corpus has that shape, so the
          corpus does not object to it either.
          ADJ, counted directly against the three files: every `<` written
          as a comparison has a space on both sides -- 36 of them, e.g.
          unified.z:224 "if (a < b)" and ecs.z:652 "if (d < bd)" -- and
          every `<` written as a compile-time bracket has no space on
          either side -- 274 of them, e.g. ecs.z:519 "ptr<System<M>>". The
          split is 310 for 310. Whitespace is not a rule of this language
          and no source document mentions it, so what the corpus actually
          exercises is one author's spacing habit. See Z-45.

FOUND BY  PRS. One source. Z-03 raised the ambiguity; this issue is about
          the proposed answer to it, and is filed separately so that
          closing Z-03 does not silently close this.

BLOCKS    Everything Z-03 blocks, and additionally any claim that a parser
          built from grammar.ebnf is correct. A parse-clean run over this
          corpus is not evidence about `<`.

OPTIONS   grammar.ebnf:386-446 is a complete and self-aware proposal: it
          states the rule, states that a token scan cannot decide the
          ambiguous case, picks the winner by fiat and gives the escape
          hatch. Adopt or reject it explicitly. Whatever is adopted needs a
          test set that is not the corpus, because the corpus contains no
          input on which the candidate rules disagree.

Z-06  A POINTEE CARRIES NO QUALIFIER AND NOTHING SAYS SO        SOUNDNESS

WHAT      `ptr<T>` is an unrestricted handle of indefinite life, no
          signature can say otherwise, and seven distinct ways to get a
          wrong answer follow from it.

EVIDENCE  rules.txt:249  "The second is the deepest hole in the design."
          rules.txt:829-838 (OPEN) states that `::`/`:=` encode mutability
          for bindings and nothing does for pointees.
          glossary.txt:397-398  "const  -- nothing yet. `::` gives
          immutable BINDINGS; there is no story for immutable POINTEES.
          Open."
          The corpus pays it in four shapes:
            ecs.z:164 `Store.at` hands out `Option<ptr<C>>` into a List
              that reallocates on the next `add` (ecs.z:171-180);
            ecs.z:617-630 `Combat.chase` holds two interior pointers into
              one store and writes through one;
            ecs.z:497-510 against ecs.z:566-574 -- `Reaper.run` iterates
              `w.hp` and emits an event whose subscriber despawns, which
              swap-removes inside `w.hp` under the loop;
            ecs.z:188 the swap-remove itself (see Z-12).

FOUND BY  OQ-0, which is the root of its dependency graph. ANX, as seven
          separate entries: U.1.1 invalidation, U.1.2 lifetime, U.1.3
          erased-borrow, U.1.4 aliasing, U.1.6 reentrancy, U.1.7
          self-referential copy, U.3.6 slice aliasing. INV, at
          rules.txt:227-229. All three inputs, nine findings, one issue.
          The framings differ usefully: OQ treats it as one undecided bit;
          ANX treats it as seven permanent holes to be documented. Both are
          right, in that order -- ANX's seven become permanent only if the
          bit comes back "no".

BLOCKS    Z-09 (the constructor body's hardest case is
          `stores.push(&self.pos)`), Z-25 (a traversal form has to say what
          happens when the body mutates the thing traversed), Z-14, and the
          language's central claim that dispatch and cost are visible in
          the type.

OPTIONS   OQ-0: split one bit off -- "does a pointee carry a qualifier at
          all, yes or no" -- and decide that before the full aliasing
          design. A "no" closes seven annex entries as permanent and
          unblocks Z-09 immediately; a "yes" opens the design space.

Z-07  NO OVERFLOW, DIVISION OR SHIFT RULE, AND THE STDLIB
      ALREADY DEPENDS ON ONE ANSWER                             SOUNDNESS

WHAT      What arithmetic does when a result is not representable, when a
          divisor is zero, or when a shift count is out of range, is not
          stated, and the standard library has already committed to
          wrapping without saying so.

EVIDENCE  rules.txt:454-457 is the whole of the arithmetic rule:
          "ARITHMETIC AND COMPARISON ARE INSTRUCTIONS ON THE PRIMITIVES,
          and they desugar to nothing. ... work on u8, u32, i32, f32, f64
          and bool, and nowhere else." Nothing follows about behaviour.
          Already depended on:
            json.z:104-106  "h := 14695981039346656037" then
              "h = (h ^ k.ptr[i]) * 1099511628211"  -- FNV-1a requires the
              multiply to wrap;
            ecs.z:128  "hash   = (k) -> u64  { (k.id * 2654435761)
              ^ (k.gen * 40503) }" -- the product exceeds u32 for almost
              every input.
          Already a hazard:
            unified.z:92  "allocator.alloc(n * size_of<T>())" -- an
              overflow here under-allocates and every element write after
              it is out of bounds;
            unified.z:181  "new_cap   := self.capacity * 2";
            json.z:152  "i    := KY.hash(k) % self.cap" -- a modulo on
              every lookup, with no rule for a zero divisor.
          rules.txt does contain the words "saturating" and "wrapping",
          once each, at rules.txt:775-776, and glossary.txt:215 has them
          too -- but only as the example that makes ADDITION A CHOICE and
          therefore a policy. Neither is a rule about primitive arithmetic.
          See CLAIMS, items 1 and 2.

FOUND BY  ANX (U.5.1 overflow, U.5.2 divzero, U.5.3 shift) and OQ-4.
          Independently: ANX reached it by enumerating what the spec does
          not check; OQ reached it by noticing the corpus had already
          decided. ANX calls it "the largest single gap in the
          specification"; OQ calls it "a design question wearing a spec
          gap's clothes". The second framing is the more useful one --
          this is not drafting, because the plausible answers differ.

BLOCKS    Any claim that a zlang program means one thing. Z-08, since a
          narrowing conversion is the same decision.

OPTIONS   OQ-4 lists four: wrap, trap, checked-build-traps /
          release-wraps, and undefined. It does not choose.

Z-08  THE PRIMITIVE LIST OMITS u64 AND THERE IS NO CONVERSION
      RULE, WHILE THE CORPUS DOES BOTH                          SOUNDNESS

WHAT      rules.txt names six types arithmetic works on "and nowhere else",
          the corpus does arithmetic on a seventh, and mixes widths in one
          expression with no rule permitting it.

EVIDENCE  rules.txt:455-456  "... work on u8, u32, i32, f32, f64 and bool,
          and nowhere else."
          u64 in the corpus, four sites:
            json.z:96   "    hash:   (k: K) -> u64"
            json.z:103  "    hash = (k) -> u64 {"
            json.z:123  "    hash   = (k) -> u64  { fnv_lower(k) }"
            ecs.z:128   u32 operands, u64 result
          Mixed widths with no conversion rule:
            json.z:152  a u64 hash reduced modulo a u32 capacity;
            ecs.z:785-787 invents a function and says so: "`f32_of` is
              invented. Neither file has a story for numeric conversion,

Z-43  THERE IS NO BARE BLOCK, SO NOTHING CAN OPEN A SCOPE --
      AND `defer` FIRES AT SCOPE EXIT                           GAP

WHAT      `{ x := 1 }` in a function body does not derive. A brace opens a
          declaration body, a control-flow body, a match arm or a block
          literal, and nothing else, so a program has no way to open a
          scope for its own sake. The language nevertheless has a construct
          whose entire definition is a scope boundary.

EVIDENCE  PRS, and grammar.ebnf:501-512 now records it under KNOWN
          UNDER-ACCEPTANCE -- a language-design finding, not a grammar bug:
          "Block is not an Operand, and the only `{` an expression may open
          is a BlockLit, whose FieldFill requires `name = expr`. So the
          language has no way to open a scope."
          glossary.txt:366 is the whole of `defer`: "defer  run at scope
          exit". Z-23 records that nothing says whether "scope" means the
          enclosing block or the enclosing declaration. Without a bare
          block the only scopes a programmer can introduce are a function
          body and the body of an `if`, `while`, `for`, `loop` or match
          arm -- but no document states that, and the gloss is written as
          though a reader could open one.
          The corpus does not settle it. All five `defer`s in the corpus
          sit at the top of a function body -- unified.z:339, unified.z:363,
          unified.z:370, ecs.z:754, ecs.z:769 -- so every one has the same
          meaning under both readings and none of them exercises a narrower
          scope.
          Related: Z-09, a `-> self` block has no statement position at
          all, which is why ecs.z:342-349 is two-phase. A bare block is one
          of the things a statement position would have to decide about.

FOUND BY  PRS for the derivation, ADJ for the consequence. A gap in the
          language and not a bug in grammar.ebnf, which is why the grammar
          records it rather than inventing a production. Adding one is a
          line; the reason not to add it unilaterally is that `{` is
          already spoken for -- declaration body, control-flow body, match
          arm, and the construction form `Option<u8>{ some = 3 }`
          (rules.txt:124-125) -- so deciding that a brace in statement
          position opens a scope is a decision about all of them.

BLOCKS    Z-23, which cannot state `defer` semantics without knowing what a
          scope is. Part of Z-01, which must either give the statement
          layer a form for this or state that there is none.

OPTIONS   No candidate resolution in the sources. Neither rules.txt nor
          glossary.txt contains a bare block and the corpus contains none,
          so there is nothing to adopt or reject: this one has to be
          decided rather than found.

Z-28  `_` IS DEFINED AS ONE IDEA AND IS THREE                   DEFECT
      -- CLOSED by the same change that closed Z-04.

CLOSED    Half of this issue was that `_` is not a hole. That half is now
          settled by construction: a hole is marked `:?` and none of the
          four uses of `_` carries one, so the two are different marks for
          different acts and the definition can no longer be stated in
          terms of a hole.

          survive re-reading once the first was fixed. "A name declined"
          covers all four uses including the compile-time parameter:
          declining to name a parameter still creates it, which is exactly
          why two `_` are two independent parameters. That sentence was
          unintelligible only under the hole reading.

          three-way split was NOT taken; wild.arm.1, wild.payload.1 and
          wild.discard.1 already state the three positions separately, so
          the split existed and only the definition above them was wrong.
          What remains is narrower and is now an [UNSPECIFIED] at
          wild.def.1: whether `_` in an expression position is an error.

          anonymous compile-time parameter, and the document proves this
          itself two dozen lines later.

EVIDENCE  rules.txt:415-421
              `_` IS A HOLE IN A BINDING POSITION, and that is its whole
              definition. Four uses, one idea:
                  _        { ... }        a wildcard arm
                  some (_) { ... }        an ignored payload
                  ptr<List<T, _>>         a compile-time arg you decline

WHAT THE CORPUS PROVES
============================================================================

unified.z, json.z and ecs.z are the only evidence about this language that
is not an opinion. Every claim below was counted or read out of the three
files during this consolidation, not taken from an input. Where a claim
settles something the prose gets wrong, the prose line is named.

 1. `break` IS NEVER NEEDED. Zero occurrences in 2,483 lines of .z. It
    appears only in the keyword budget (rules.txt:352, glossary.txt:367)
    and in the grammar that had to invent a production for it
    (grammar.ebnf:371). `continue` is used four times (json.z:585-587,
    ecs.z:648); `loop` four times; `while` six. A keyword budget "written
    down so it stops growing by accident" (rules.txt:348) contains a word
    the worked source has never once wanted.

 2. THE DOCUMENTED CONSTRUCTION FORM IS NEVER USED. `Option<u8>{ some = 3 }`
    (rules.txt:124-125) has zero occurrences. The derived-function form the
    same paragraph presents as a consequence -- `Option<u8>.some(3)` -- has
    54 qualified uses plus 42 unqualified ones. The rule stated as primary
    is unexercised; the rule stated as a corollary is the language.

 3. u64 ARITHMETIC HAPPENS DESPITE u64 BEING ABSENT FROM THE PRIMITIVE
    LIST. rules.txt:455-456 says the operators work on six types "and
    nowhere else". `Key.hash` projects `u64` (json.z:96), FNV-1a xors and
    multiplies on it (json.z:104-106), and ecs.z:128 combines two u32
    operands into a u64 result. The document then quotes that same hash
    expression at rules.txt:516 as the argument for how deref is spelled.

 4. THE STANDARD LIBRARY REQUIRES WRAPPING MULTIPLICATION AND NO RULE SAYS
    SO. json.z:106 `h = (h ^ k.ptr[i]) * 1099511628211` and ecs.z:128
    `k.id * 2654435761` produce the values they intend only if the multiply
    wraps. rules.txt says nothing about overflow anywhere; its only uses of
    the words "saturating" and "wrapping" (rules.txt:775-776,
    glossary.txt:215) are an example of addition being a choice.

 5. 28 THREE-CLAUSE `for` LOOPS EXIST AND rules.txt HAS NO FORM FOR THEM.
    Every one is `for (init; cond; step)`. rules.txt names `for` once, in a
    keyword list, and never gives a syntax. The same is true of `while`,
    `loop`, `if`, `return`, `continue` and `defer`. grammar.ebnf:353-382
    invented the lot. (The count is 28, not 32: four further occurrences of
    "for (" are comments about forms that do not exist. See CLAIMS.)

 6. `[]` MEANS FIVE THINGS, AND TWO OF THEM ARE IN THE SAME DECLARATION.
    `List` writes `new_items[i] = self.items[i]` (unified.z:183, raw
    pointer, element, no `.*`) and declares
    `[] :: (&self, i: u32) -> ptr<T>` (unified.z:198), which requires
    `xs[i].* = v` for the same write. Map's `[]` projects `Option<V>`
    (json.z:208). A slice's `[]` projects the element (unified.z:277).
    `Store.at` projects `Option<ptr<C>>` (ecs.z:164). No rule chooses.

 7. THE `-> oneof` RULES ALL HOLD. Every sum in the corpus -- `Option`
    (unified.z:21), `Result` (unified.z:26), `Slot` (json.z:138), `Json`
    (json.z:243), `Token` (json.z:257), `Ai` (ecs.z:262) -- has no runtime
    group, as rules.txt:113-115 requires, and three of the six carry
    compile-time arguments. This is the one substantial block of rules the
    corpus fully confirms.

 8. A BODILESS DECLARATION IS A RECORD, NOT A HOLE, WHEN IT HAS A BINDER.
    json.z:77, json.z:136, json.z:268, ecs.z:258 and ecs.z:413 are all
    bodiless, all have `::`, and all are records. rules.txt:132 says
    bodiless means hole and never mentions the binder.

 9. APPLYING COMPILE-TIME ARGUMENTS PRODUCES VALUES AS OFTEN AS TYPES.
    `json_fmt<ptr<Arena>>` (json.z:653), `spaced<ptr<Arena>>` (json.z:654),
    `dump<>` and `dump<indent = 4>` (ecs.z:802-803),
    `list_fmt<F = fmt_u8>` (unified.z:348) are all values. rules.txt:32
    says a type.

10. A CONTAINER THAT CANNOT BE ITERATED CANNOT CLOSE ITS PROJECTION.
    `List` projects six names (unified.z:157) because `[]` exists. `Map`
    projects `-> self` (json.z:132) because two formatters must read
    `members.slots` and `members.cap` to iterate (json.z:487-488,
    json.z:539-540). One rule, one confirming case, one refuting case.

11. THE PROJECTION IS AN EXPORT LIST AND NOT A READ-ONLY ONE. `count` is a
    `:=` field in `List`'s projection (unified.z:157-159), and ecs.z:501
    writes it: `self.doomed.count = 0`, resetting a container by its
    length. Legal under every rule as written.

12. FOUR NAMES THE CORPUS CALLS DO NOT EXIST. `size_of` (unified.z:92),
    `slice(p, n)` (unified.z:200), `parse_f64` (json.z:338) and `page`
    (unified.z:367) are used and declared nowhere; `tag_of` (json.z:383)
    and `f32_of` (ecs.z:785) are used and admitted as inventions in the
    files' own comments; `nil` (unified.z:191) is in no keyword list and no
    declaration. Seven names, four files, no home.

13. THE CORPUS IS WRITTEN IN A DIALECT THE RULES DO NOT DESCRIBE. 42 bare
    `ok(`/`err(`/`some(`/`none(` calls need a type inferred outward from
    the enclosing projection (json.z:291 and thirteen more in the lexer
    alone). rules.txt:540-550 licenses inference from arguments and from an
    expected type, and never from a projection into a body.

14. TWO-PHASE CONSTRUCTION IS NOT A STYLE CHOICE. ecs.z:342-349 exists
    because a `-> self` block has no statement position; ecs.z:753-755
    records that forgetting the second call makes `despawn` silently do
    nothing; json.z:349-357 takes the other dodge and pays a match on every
    `peek`. Two independent programs, two different workarounds, one hole.

15. THE ERASED-COERCION QUESTION IS GENUINELY OPEN, BECAUSE THE CORPUS DOES
    BOTH. Annotated: unified.z:368 `boxed : ptr<Allocator> := &arena`,
    unified.z:392 `f : ptr<Fn<u8, ()>> := &p`. Implicit: unified.z:327
    `F.format(v, &b)`, json.z:691 `each_child(doc, &tally(&n))`, ecs.z:779
    `w.died.on(&obituary)`. rules.txt:892 names the first implicit site
    itself, inside the OPEN item that asks the question.
