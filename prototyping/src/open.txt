==============================================================================
open.txt  --  the questions the design has not answered
==============================================================================

WHAT IS STILL UNDECIDED, and nothing else. It states no rule and retires no
hole. Rules: spec.txt, which is the sole normative document. Syntax:
grammar.ebnf. Argument: rationale.txt. rules.txt is the superseded
original, retained frozen so that the line citations here stay valid; it is
not to be cited as current.

Every item carries an IN THE SPEC. line giving the spec.txt rule
identifiers the question hangs off, or saying that no rule corresponds and
naming the issue in issues.txt that tracks the gap. Each item is also cited
in spec.txt at the point where the question arises, as `>>> [OPEN oq.N]`.

A design log. Every item here is genuinely undecided: the design does not
have an answer, and the answer will change the language. Anything already
decided -- including decided holes, of which there are forty-nine -- belongs
in unchecked.txt and not here.

Items are ordered by how much of the rest of the language each one blocks,
which is not the order they were discovered in and not the order rules.txt
gives. The argument for the order is in the last section.

Each item carries a stable identifier, the question in one line, what the
corpus pays instead today with file:line evidence, what the answer unblocks,
what it depends on, and which annex entries it would retire.

NOT IN THIS LOG. Eleven gaps in the specification are not design questions:
the answer is not in dispute, it has simply never been written down. They are
unchecked.txt U.1.5, U.1.8, U.1.9, U.2.5, U.3.4, U.6.3, U.10.1, U.10.2,
U.10.3, U.11.1 and U.11.3 -- aliasing assumptions, null, pointer arithmetic,
allocator preconditions, bounds, alignment, `defer` semantics, operand order,
recursion depth, layout, and compile-time effects. Those are drafting, and
none of them should acquire an entry here. Compile-time termination, U.11.2,
was in that list and is not: OQ-8 reaches part of it.

OQ-0  `oq.ptr.mutability`

QUESTION. Does a pointee carry a qualifier -- mutability, validity, or both
-- or does `ptr<T>` remain a single unrestricted handle of indefinite life?

This is one bit, and it is prior to the rest of the aliasing question.
Deciding yes opens a large design space (what the qualifier is, where it is
written, whether it is inferred, what it costs). Deciding no closes seven
annex entries as permanent and unblocks OQ-1 immediately.

WHAT HAPPENS INSTEAD TODAY. `::` and `:=` encode immutable and mutable for
bindings and nothing does for pointees, so every `ptr<T>` is fully mutable.
The corpus pays this in four separate shapes:

  - `Store.at` hands out a `ptr<C>` into a `List` that reallocates on the
    next `add` -- ecs.z:164-169, valid until anyone adds a component of that
    type, with nothing in the signature saying so.

  - `Combat.chase` holds `me` and `prey`, two interior pointers into the same
    store, obtained from two lookups -- ecs.z:617-618 -- and writes through
    two further pointers into two other stores in the same body, at
    ecs.z:623 and ecs.z:630. Nothing in any of the four signatures says which
    of them may alias.

  - `Signal.emit` calls every subscriber while the emitter is mid-operation
    -- ecs.z:557-561 -- and a subscriber may hold a pointer to the container
    the caller is walking. Three signatures, no reentrancy in any of them.

  - `Map.get` and `Map.put` both take `&self` -- json.z:172 and json.z:165 --
    and nothing says `get` does not write. json.z:831-836 states the gap.

WHAT IT BLOCKS. The language's central claim, which is that dispatch and cost
are visible in the type. Aliasing is the one cost that is not
(rules.txt:838). Downstream: `const` has no story at all (glossary.txt:398);
a container accessor cannot promise anything about the pointer it returns;
an `off` on an observer list is not the only API the language cannot express.

IN THE SPEC. spec.txt [mem.nomove.1], [mem.bindaddr.1], [mem.interior.1],
[ptr.typed.1]. The question is cited there as `>>> [OPEN oq.0]`.

DEPENDS ON. Nothing. It is the root.

RETIRES. U.1.1 (invalidation), U.1.2 (lifetime), U.1.4 (aliasing), U.1.6
(reentrancy), U.3.6 (slice aliasing); possibly U.1.3 and U.1.7 depending on
how far the qualifier reaches.

NOTE. rules.txt calls this "the deepest hole in the design" at line 249 and
does not schedule it. Splitting the one-bit question out of the full aliasing
design is the point of this entry: the bit is answerable now, and OQ-1 needs
it.

NOTE ON THE EVIDENCE. ecs.z:897-916 (FRICTION 25) states two of the bullets
above more strongly than its own code supports. It says `Combat.chase`
"writes through one of" `me` and `prey`; both are only read, at ecs.z:620.
It says a closure holding `ptr<World<M>>` despawns under `Reaper`'s loop over
`w.hp`; the closure actually written, `on_death` at ecs.z:572-574, captures a
`ptr<List<Entity,M>>`, and `Reaper.run` collects in one loop and emits in a
second for exactly that reason (ecs.z:498-510). The hazards are real and
unexpressible either way. The corpus pays for them by hand rather than
exhibiting them.

OQ-1  `oq.ctor.body`

QUESTION. What gives a `-> self` block a statement position, and where does
`self` become available inside it?

WHAT HAPPENS INSTEAD TODAY. Two-phase construction, unchecked. A second
member does everything the block cannot, and the caller must remember it:

  - `World.wire` -- ecs.z:342-349 -- pushes `&self.pos`, `&self.vel`,
    `&self.hp`, `&self.brain` into `self.stores`, guarded by a `wired` flag
    that exists only to make the omission idempotent rather than detectable.

  - `main` calls it on the line after construction -- ecs.z:753-755 -- with
    the comment "Forget it and `despawn` silently does nothing at all."

  - json.z takes the other dodge: `Parser` cannot prime its lookahead at
    construction because priming can fail, so `look` is an `Option<Token>`
    filled lazily and every `peek` pays a match -- json.z:349-373. The file
    records the alternative it did not take (prime at the call site and pass
    the token in) at json.z:353-357.

WHAT IT BLOCKS. Anything needing `self`, two fields at once, or a step that
can fail. Concretely: self-referential structures, which the memory rules
explicitly permit (rules.txt:256-259) and the syntax cannot build; fallible
construction; and any allocation-failure story, since a constructor that
allocates has nowhere to report the failure.

IN THE SPEC. spec.txt [mem.construct.1], [bind.which.self.1]. The question is
cited there as `>>> [OPEN oq.1]`.

DEPENDS ON. OQ-0, softly but really. [P21]'s worked example is
`stores.push(&self.pos)` -- taking a mutable interior pointer to a
partially-constructed value and storing it where it outlives the constructor.
That is the single hardest case any pointee qualifier will have to
adjudicate. Specifying the body first means specifying it around an example
whose legality the aliasing answer has not yet settled.

RETIRES. U.4.1 (two-phase), U.4.2 (`undefined` fields, which exist mainly
because there is no body to fill them in), U.4.3 (initialiser order, which a
statement position makes textual).

PRIOR ART IN THIS REPO. [P21] in ecs.z:1063-1088 is a complete proposal:
statements interleaved with `:=` bindings, run in textual order; `self`
available at the point every `:=` field is bound; `-> Result<self, E>` folded
in rather than standing alone. The fallible projection was tried separately
and cut -- rules.txt:844-853 and ecs.z:918-928 give the reason, which is that
a return type is not a body.

OQ-2  `oq.sum.discriminant`      -- ANSWERED: YES

ANSWER. Yes. [P19] adopted whole, with one deviation: the rule spells a tag
variant `Token.tag.colon()` and [P19] spells it `Token.tag.colon`, because a
tag's variants are constructed by hole.ctor.1 like every other variant and
`Option<T>.none()` already spells a payloadless one with parentheses. Stated
at spec.txt sum.tag.type.1 through sum.tag.stable.1, under A SUM'S
DISCRIMINANT. Two things [P19] left implicit are now stated: `S.tag.tag` is
`S.tag`, and an ordinal is declaration order and nothing versions it. The
entry below is kept as it was written, so that what was asked can still be
read against what was answered. See issues.txt Z-20.

QUESTION. Is a sum's discriminant a value? [P19] proposes `S.tag` as a
derived sum of unit variants, with `tag_of(v)` total, comparable, ordinal,
and usable as an array index or a bit position.

WHAT HAPPENS INSTEAD TODAY. A full match for every question about a tag:

  - `is_dead` spells four arms to compute a bool -- ecs.z:272-274.
  - `Parser.expect` invents `tag_of` because the language has no spelling for
    "same variant, ignore the payload" -- json.z:381-387, marked FRICTION 7.
  - `Map.get` writes `empty` and `dead` arms it can prove unreachable --
    json.z:175-179.
  - An archetype bitmask, which is what a real ECS is built from, cannot be
    written at all: it needs one bit per component type and therefore an
    ordinal per variant -- ecs.z:275-278.
  - Serialisation has no stable number to write -- same site.

WHAT IT BLOCKS. A data structure, not a convenience. Both programs hit it,
and ecs.z upgraded it from json.z's rating on exactly that ground
(ecs.z:1159-1160: "no ordinal, no bitmask, no archetypes, no
serialisation").

IN THE SPEC. spec.txt [decl.proj.oneof.1], [hole.oneof.variant.1]. The
question is cited there as `>>> [OPEN oq.2]`.

DEPENDS ON. Nothing. It touches no other open question and no decided rule --
HOLES already makes a variant a derived function, so a derived sum of unit
variants is the same move read once more.

RETIRES. U.7.2 (sum representation unspecified).

NOTE. This is the cheapest large win in the log: fully specified in [P19]
(ecs.z:1142-1148), zero dependencies, closes frictions in both programs, and
changes no existing spelling.

OQ-3  `oq.iter.form`

QUESTION. `for (x := it)` over anything with a `next` returning `Option<T>`
-- and what the form quantifies over. [P23] proposes the desugaring; what it
ranges over is the open half.

WHAT HAPPENS INSTEAD TODAY. Every traversal in both programs is a C index
loop. The standard library's attempt to answer this without the form --
`Iterator<T>`, `ListIter`, `iter`, `fold` -- was declared by both programs
and is cut (rules.txt:865-870, ecs.z:968-974). The hand-written consumer is
four lines to replace one:

  - `Join2` has a `next` returning `Option<Hit<A,B>>` -- ecs.z:415-433 -- and
    every user writes the `loop` + `match` consumer out by hand:
    `Movement.run` at ecs.z:466-479 is four lines where
    `for (h := join2(&w.pos, &w.vel))` is one.
  - Everything with no `next` at all is a C index loop: ecs.z:487-493
    (`Decay.run`), ecs.z:502-510 (`Reaper.run`, twice), ecs.z:586-599
    (`Combat.run`), json.z:686 and json.z:477-480.

[UNVERIFIED -- citation not confirmed] How much the cut shape was used is
recorded twice and inconsistently. rules.txt:867-868 says `Iterator` was
"declared by both programs, used by neither". ecs.z:970 says it "was used
exactly once -- by `fold`, in json.z". Neither the shape nor `fold` survives
in json.z as it stands, so no call site can be checked from the corpus. The
argument does not turn on the number.

WHAT IT BLOCKS. A visibility property of the whole standard library. `Map`
cannot close its projection, because iterating a `Map` means reaching into
`slots` and `cap` -- json.z:487-488, ecs.z:930-942 (FRICTION 17). `List`
closed its projection only because `[]` arrived. So this question decides
whether VISIBILITY IS THE PROJECTION is a rule containers can actually obey.

IN THE SPEC. spec.txt: none -- see issues.txt Z-25. The question is cited in
spec.txt as `>>> [OPEN oq.3]`.

DEPENDS ON. OQ-0. The traversal form has to say what happens when the body
mutates the thing being traversed, and that is the aliasing question at a
specific site.

RETIRES. Nothing in the annex. In particular it does not retire U.3.5
(mutation during traversal): a desugared loop reads the same count from the
same container and skips the same swap-removed element. It removes the syntax
cost, not the hazard.

OQ-4  `oq.num.semantics`

QUESTION. What do arithmetic overflow, division by zero, and out-of-range
shift do?

WHAT HAPPENS INSTEAD TODAY. Nothing is written. No rule in rules.txt or
glossary.txt defines overflow, integer conversion, or division by zero, and
the words `overflow`, `trap`, `divide`, `division` and `convert` do not occur
in either file. `saturating` and `wrapping` do occur, at rules.txt:775-776
and glossary.txt:215, but only as the example of why ADDITION IS A CHOICE
that wants a policy -- in the argument whose conclusion is that arithmetic is
an instruction on the primitives and there is no policy for it. Neither line
says what a primitive `+` does. Meanwhile:

  - the standard library's FNV-1a hash requires wrapping multiplication to
    compute the value it intends -- json.z:104-108, a `u64` accumulator with
    an offset basis and prime that are defined modulo 2^64;
  - `mem.make` allocates `n * size_of<T>()` -- unified.z:91-93 -- so an
    overflow there under-allocates silently and every element write
    afterwards is out of bounds;
  - `List.expand` doubles a capacity -- unified.z:181;
  - `entity_key.hash` at ecs.z:128 multiplies `u32` operands into a `u64`
    projection, which overflows under one reading and does not under the
    other, and computes a different hash each way. That one is as much OQ-5
    as OQ-4, which is the argument for deciding them together.

WHAT IT BLOCKS. Any claim that a zlang program means one thing. It also
blocks OQ-5, since a conversion rule has to say what a narrowing conversion
does and that is the same decision.

IN THE SPEC. spec.txt [op.instructions.1], [op.primtypes.2]. The question is
cited there as `>>> [OPEN oq.4]`.

DEPENDS ON. Nothing.

RETIRES. U.5.1, U.5.2, U.5.3.

NOTE. This item is here rather than in the drafting list because it is not
merely unwritten: the plausible answers differ (wrap, trap,
checked-build-traps-release-wraps, undefined), and the standard library has
already committed to one of them without saying so. That is a design question
wearing a spec gap's clothes.

OQ-5  `oq.num.conversion`

QUESTION. How do the six primitive types convert into one another? ecs.z's
guess is "one more policy, `From<A, B>`" -- ecs.z:785-786.

WHAT HAPPENS INSTEAD TODAY. The corpus mixes widths with no rule permitting
it, and invents a function where it cannot:

  - `Map.probe` reduces a `u64` hash modulo a `u32` capacity -- json.z:152;
  - `entity_key.hash` combines two `u32` operands into a `u64` result --
    ecs.z:128;
  - `main` calls `f32_of(i)` on a loop counter, and the comment says the
    function is invented -- ecs.z:785-787.

WHAT IT BLOCKS. Numeric code generally, and the honesty of the primitive
list: rules.txt:456 names six types that arithmetic works on and no way to
get between them.

IN THE SPEC. spec.txt [op.primtypes.1], [op.primtypes.2]. The question is
cited there as `>>> [OPEN oq.5]`.

DEPENDS ON. OQ-4 (narrowing is overflow), and interacts with OQ-6 -- if
conversion is a policy, it is the second policy the language ships and the
first that a generic body would want.

RETIRES. U.5.4, U.5.5.

OQ-6  `oq.numeric.generic`

QUESTION. Can generic code be numeric?
`lerp :: <T> (a: T, b: T, t: f32) -> T` cannot be written, because a generic
body has no `+`.

WHAT HAPPENS INSTEAD TODAY. A function per type, whose body reads as a chain
rather than as a formula:

  - `lerp` is monomorphic on `Vec2` and spelled `a.add(b.sub(a).scale(t))` --
    ecs.z:101, with the comment at ecs.z:97-99 calling it "the one thing in
    this file with no replacement at all";
  - the integration step in `Movement.run` is `h.a.*.add(h.b.*.scale(dt))` --
    ecs.z:477, against the `h.a.* + h.b.* * dt` it used to be;
  - `2.0 * v` has no spelling in any form.

WHAT IT BLOCKS. Numeric libraries. The available answer is a shape with `add`
and `scale`, which serves types you own, hits the no-adapter problem
(rules.txt:624-631) for anything else, and cannot serve the primitives at all
-- nothing can be added to `u8`.

IN THE SPEC. spec.txt [op.instructions.1], [op.notoperators.1],
[op.usertype.1]. The question is cited there as `>>> [OPEN oq.6]`.

DEPENDS ON. OQ-5 conceptually. Blocked by a decision already taken: the
solver is gone, and [P17] -- operators as policies -- was rejected with it
(ecs.z:1015-1061). Any answer here must work without a pool, which is what
makes it hard rather than merely unwritten.

RETIRES. Nothing in the annex. This is a missing capability, not a hole.

OQ-7  `oq.alloc.failure`

QUESTION. Can an allocator fail, and if so how does it say so?

WHAT HAPPENS INSTEAD TODAY. `Allocator` declares
`alloc: (&self, n: u32) -> ptr` -- unified.z:81-84 -- which has no room for a
failure. So:

  - `mem.make` returns the result unexamined -- unified.z:91-93;
  - every allocation in both programs is used without a check --
    unified.z:164, unified.z:182, json.z:146;
  - `Arena.alloc` advances `top` by `n` and returns without comparing
    anything to `size` -- unified.z:104-112, where `size` is a field the
    block never reads. It is not that the arena forgot to check; it is that
    there is nowhere to put the answer.

WHAT IT BLOCKS. Any program that must survive exhaustion, and the whole class
of fixed-extent allocators the language otherwise encourages.

IN THE SPEC. spec.txt: none -- see issues.txt Z-10. The question is cited in
spec.txt as `>>> [OPEN oq.7]`.

DEPENDS ON. OQ-1. A constructor that allocates -- which is every container --
needs a body with a failure path before `alloc` returning an `Option<ptr>` is
usable. `List :: <T, M> (allocator: M) -> self` with
`items := mem.make(...)` has nowhere to handle a `none`.

RETIRES. U.6.1, U.6.2.

OQ-8  `oq.mono.recursive`

QUESTION. What does monomorphisation do to a type that contains itself?

WHAT HAPPENS INSTEAD TODAY. The alias trick, applied once per application:
`Doc :: Json<ptr<Arena>>` (json.z:649) and `A :: ptr<Arena>` (ecs.z:750). Two
unrelated programs reached for the same one-line fix, which makes it a
pattern (rules.txt NOTE 2 683-693, ecs.z:884-888).

The residual cost is one allocator per document type: `Json<Heap>` and
`Json<Arena>` cannot be compared, merged, or stored in one list --
json.z:778-783. And the alias does not reach a library shape: `System<M>`
stays generic because a library cannot name the application's allocator --
ecs.z:450-455.

WHAT IT BLOCKS. Recursive generic data structures, and the reach of the alias
workaround into libraries.

IN THE SPEC. spec.txt [abs.bound.1]. The question is cited there as `>>> [OPEN
oq.8]`.

DEPENDS ON. Nothing.

RETIRES. U.11.2, in part -- a decision here would say what a non-terminating
instantiation does.

NOTE. json.z's closing verdict names this as one of exactly two things
standing between the design and a usable language, and says of it "it is not
syntax" -- json.z:879-887. The other one is done.

OQ-11  `oq.shape.coercion`

QUESTION. Is the coercion to `ptr<Shape>` implicit at a call site, or does it
need the annotation, as `boxed : ptr<Allocator> := &arena` does?

WHAT HAPPENS INSTEAD TODAY. Both spellings are in the corpus and the rules

  - Annotated, at a binding: unified.z:368 (`boxed : ptr<Allocator> :=

ecs.z:775-777 calls `&obituary` "the coercion ... exactly as
`boxed : ptr<Allocator>` is in unified.z", but the two sites are written in
the two different forms this question is about.

WHAT IT BLOCKS. Whether a reader can tell a direct call from an indirect one
by reading the call, which is the property the bracket split exists to
provide (rules.txt:618-622). Implicit is nicer to write and hides a
representation change.

IN THE SPEC. spec.txt [abs.erased.1], [abs.nodyn.1]. The question is cited
there as `>>> [OPEN oq.11]`.

DEPENDS ON. Nothing.

RETIRES. U.9.3.

OQ-15  `oq.match.binder-sugar`

QUESTION. A single-arm binding form with an `else`, for the tail that
repeats. Swift spells it `guard`, Rust `let ... else`.

WHAT HAPPENS INSTEAD TODAY. Two tails, written out.
`{ arm (v) { v }  _ { return } }` appears at json.z:679-681 and four times in
`Combat.chase` (ecs.z:617-618, ecs.z:637, ecs.z:643);
`{ ok (v) { v }  err (e) { return err(e) } }` appears at all fourteen
propagation sites in json.z (json.z:370, 376, 382, 390, 408, 411, 413, 425,
428, 433-436, 437, 439, 456, 457), and rules.txt:411-413 says json.z "is
where you can see whether that reads as honest or as noise".

THE ORDER, AND WHETHER rules.txt HAS IT RIGHT

rules.txt schedules exactly one thing. At line 854 it says of the constructor
body: "A value having one address is what makes such a body definable at all,
so this is the next thing to decide."

That is nearly right and it is right for the wrong reason, and the document
contradicts itself six hundred lines earlier: MEMORY, at line 249, calls
aliasing "the deepest hole in the design". Both statements can be true --
deepest is not next -- but the document never says why the deepest hole is
not the next one, and the reason it gives for scheduling the constructor body
is about a prerequisite being satisfied rather than about what the decision
unblocks.

THREE AMENDMENTS.

ONE: SPLIT ONE BIT OFF THE ALIASING QUESTION AND DECIDE THAT BIT FIRST. The
full aliasing design is large and may take a year. The question "does a
pointee carry a qualifier at all -- yes or no" is answerable now, and OQ-1
needs the answer. [P21]'s worked example is `stores.push(&self.pos)`: taking
a mutable interior pointer to a value that is still being constructed and
storing it where it outlives the constructor. That is the hardest case a
pointee qualifier will ever face. Specify the constructor body first and you
specify it around an example whose legality the aliasing rule has not
settled -- and the reverse dependency does not exist, because the aliasing
answer does not need a constructor body to be stated. If the bit comes back
no -- which is consistent with the language's stated character, since it has
already refused a borrow checker and move semantics -- then OQ-1 is genuinely
next and can proceed in isolation, and this amendment costs a week.

TWO: OQ-2, THE DISCRIMINANT, IS A BETTER SECOND THAN THE CONSTRUCTOR BODY ON
PURE RETURN. -- TAKEN, and answered ahead of the order below; see the head of
OQ-2 and issues.txt Z-20. The argument is left standing because it is the
reason it was taken. It is fully specified already ([P19]), has zero dependencies,
changes no existing spelling, and blocks a data structure rather than a
convenience -- no ordinal, no bitmask, no archetypes, no serialisation. The
constructor body is a larger change with a soft dependency on OQ-0. Deciding
OQ-2 while OQ-0 is being settled costs nothing and closes frictions in both
programs.

THREE: THE ARITHMETIC QUESTIONS (OQ-4, OQ-5) ARE SCHEDULED NOWHERE AND SHOULD
BE. rules.txt does not list them in OPEN because it does not know they are
open -- the subject has never been raised, and the two occurrences of
`saturating` and `wrapping` in the specification (rules.txt:775-776,
glossary.txt:215) are an argument about policies rather than a statement
about arithmetic. But the standard library's hash already depends on wrapping
multiplication (json.z:106) and the corpus already mixes integer widths in
three places (json.z:152, ecs.z:128, ecs.z:787). This is not a gap waiting
for a language feature; it is a commitment already made in code and not yet
made in prose. It should be decided before anything else acquires a
dependency on it.
