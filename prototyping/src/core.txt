============================================================================
core.txt  --  a core language for zlang, and where the translation breaks
============================================================================

A TEST OF THE CENTRAL CLAIM, and nothing else. This file states no rule and
retires no issue. It asks one question: if module, struct, function,
procedure, sum, interface, closure and policy are all one construct read
differently, then a small core language plus a translation should exist,
and everything else should be sugar. This file writes the core, writes the
translation, and reports every place the translation needed something the
core did not have.

Read spec.txt first. Rule identifiers below are spec.txt's. Issue
identifiers are issues.txt's; open-question identifiers are open.txt's.
Where a break is already filed, the identifier is given and the break is
not re-reported as new.


============================================================================
1  SCOPE AND PURPOSE
============================================================================

NON-NORMATIVE. Nothing here constrains a program. Where this file and
spec.txt disagree, spec.txt is the specification and this file is the
report of a failed reduction.

IN SCOPE. The declaration layer: blocks, holes, projections, binders,
compile-time versus runtime application, shapes, policies, sums, closures,
visibility, receivers, and the derived constructors of a sum.

OUT OF SCOPE, AND WHY. The statement layer. `if`, `while`, `for`, `loop`,
`defer`, `return`, `break`, `continue` and assignment are not elaborated
here and no core form is offered for any of them. Z-01 records that no
source document specifies a statement or control-flow form at all: the
grammar's productions for them are marked INVENTED in spec.txt (STATEMENTS)
and were reconstructed from the corpus. A translation of an unspecified
layer would be a translation of this file's own inventions, which tests
nothing. The exclusion is not free, and section 4 records the two places
where the declaration layer cannot be closed without it (B-9, B-15).

WHAT COUNTS AS A BREAK. Three things, and each is filed in section 4:

    - the translation needed a core form that is not one construct read
      differently, but a second construct;
    - the translation needed a side condition that the surface syntax does
      not express, so the surface form is not enough to elaborate from;
    - two surface forms the design calls the same translate to different
      core terms, or one surface form translates two ways with no stated
      discriminator.


============================================================================
2  THE CORE
============================================================================

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Notation

`n` ranges over member names, `x` over variables, `k` over constants. `s`
ranges over the two stages, `c` for compile time and `r` for run time; it
is not a form, it is an index carried by three of the forms. `[[ ... ]]` is
the translation of a surface phrase.

Types and values are one syntactic category, as the design intends: a type
is a term, and which terms are types is decided by kw.type.test.1 and not
by the grammar. The single exception is E12, and E12 is a break (B-10).

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The forms

    e ::=
      E1    x                            a name
      E2    e . n                        member selection
      E3    fn^s (p, ...) e              abstraction at stage s
      E4    e @^s (e, ...)               application at stage s
      E5    rec { d, ... }               a block: a recursive record
      E6    keep e {n, ...}              restriction to a name set
      E7    sum { n : e, ... }           a tagged block
      E8    e # n                        injection into a tagged block
      E9    case e { n(x) -> e, ... }    discrimination of a tagged block
      E10   fit e e                      structural satisfaction / erasure
      E11   k                            constants and primitive operations
      E12   arrow^s ((p, ...), e)        the type of an abstraction

    d ::=
      D1    n =^s e                      a bound member
      D2    n : e                        a hole: a member with no binder

    p ::=  x : e                         a parameter

E5 binds `self` to the record itself, and binds every member name of the
record in the scope of every member. That is the fixpoint, and it is what
makes `fmt_u8.format` inside `fmt_u8` (unified.z:268) and `self.format`
inside `spaced` (json.z:526) two spellings of one thing.

E6 is written as "keep" and not as "remove" deliberately: it names what
escapes, which is what a projection does.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The count

Fourteen forms: twelve terms and two declaration forms. The brief asked for
under ten and said that needing more than ten weakens the claim. It is
weakened, and the arithmetic says by exactly how much:

    E1 E2 E3 E4 E5 E6 E10 E11 + D1 D2      ten forms
                                           module, struct, record, shape,
                                           policy type, policy instance,
                                           closure, namespace, visibility,
                                           compile-time and runtime
                                           application, bounds, erasure

    E7 E8 E9                               three more, for `-> oneof`
                                           alone (B-1)

    E12                                    one more, because a declaration
                                           and the type of a declaration
                                           are one surface production
                                           (B-10, the root of Z-04)

So the claim's own budget is met on the nose -- ten forms -- for six of the
eight things it names. It is exceeded by four, and every one of the four is
attributable to a single named cause.


============================================================================
3  THE TRANSLATIONS
============================================================================

Each translation is followed by a corpus declaration translated verbatim,
with a file:line citation, and by the rule identifiers the translation
leans on. Where a translation needs a side condition, the condition is
named S1 or S2 and filed in section 4.

Two side conditions are used throughout and are stated once here.

    S1  PARAMETER INJECTION. Under a projection that restricts (`-> self`
        or `-> {a, b}`), every runtime parameter other than the receiver
        becomes a member of the record, bound at run time to itself.
        Compile-time parameters likewise become members bound at compile
        time. Under `-> T`, `-> ()` and `-> oneof` no such injection
        happens.

    S2  RECEIVER. `self` and `&self` are not parameters in the S1 sense.
        `[[ self ]] = self : e` where e is the enclosing rec, and
        `[[ &self ]] = self : Ptr e`, by kw.self.desugar.1.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  A MODULE

    [[ N :: -> {n1, ..., nk} { B } ]]
      =  N =^c keep (rec { [[ B ]] }) {n1, ..., nk}

    [[ N :: -> self { B } ]]
      =  N =^c rec { [[ B ]] }

The second is the first with the name set taken to be every member. The
`-> self` projection is therefore not a form of its own: it is maximal
restriction. This is the first thing the core makes visible and section 5
returns to it.

WORKED EXAMPLE. unified.z:129-141.

    io :: -> {out, err, write} {
        FileWriter :: (fd: i32) -> self {
            write_bytes :: (&self, p: ptr<u8>, n: u32) -> ()
                { c_write(self.fd, p, n) }
        }
        out :: FileWriter(1)
        err :: FileWriter(2)
        write :: <W: Writer> (w: W, s: slice<u8>) -> ()
            { w.write_bytes(s.ptr, s.len) }
    }

    io =^c keep (rec {

        FileWriter =^c fn^r (fd : I32)
            rec {
                fd =^r fd,                          -- S1
                write_bytes =^c fn^r (self : Ptr self,
                                      p : Ptr @^c(U8), n : U32)
                                 c_write @^r (self.fd, p, n)
            },

        out =^c FileWriter @^r (1),
        err =^c FileWriter @^r (2),

        write =^c fn^c (W : fit W Writer)
                    fn^r (w : W, s : Slice @^c(U8))
                      (w . write_bytes) @^r (s.ptr, s.len)

    }) {out, err, write}

RELIES ON. decl.form.1, decl.form.2, decl.proj.subset.1, decl.proj.self.1,
vis.projection.1, vis.nopub.1, vis.opaque.1, bind.ct.1, kw.value.1,
decl.apply.4, abs.bound.1, abs.shape.scope.1.

NOTE. vis.opaque.1 costs nothing in the core. `keep` restricts a set of
names and does not touch the values those names hold, so `out` escapes
while `FileWriter` does not, and the type of `out` is unnameable outside
without a rule saying so. That is a genuine reduction.

NOTE. `write`'s bound `<W: Writer>` is E10 in a parameter position. Nothing
in the core distinguishes it from the fit used by a policy instance in 3.8;
see section 5.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2  A STRUCT

    [[ N :: <C1, ..., Cm> (r1: T1, ..., rn: Tn) -> {n1, ..., nk} { B } ]]
      =  N =^c fn^c (C1, ..., Cm)
                 fn^r (r1 : T1, ..., rn : Tn)
                   keep (rec { r1 =^r r1, ..., rn =^r rn,   -- S1
                               [[ B ]] })
                        {n1, ..., nk}

WORKED EXAMPLE. unified.z:157-201.

    List :: <T, M: Allocator> (allocator: M)
            -> {push, pop, count, [], span, deinit} {
        capacity := 10
        count    := 0
        items    := mem.make<T>(allocator, capacity)
        push   :: (&self, item: T) -> () { ... }
        pop    :: (&self) -> Option<T> { ... }
        expand :: (&self) -> () { ... }
        deinit :: (&self) -> () { ... }
        []     :: (&self, i: u32) -> ptr<T> { &self.items[i] }
        span   :: (&self) -> slice<T> { slice(self.items, self.count) }
    }

    List =^c fn^c (T, M : fit M Allocator)
               fn^r (allocator : M)
                 keep (rec {
                   allocator =^r allocator,          -- S1
                   capacity  =^r 10,
                   count     =^r 0,
                   items     =^r (mem . make) @^c (T)
                                             @^r (allocator, capacity),
                   push      =^c fn^r (self : Ptr self, item : T) ...,
                   pop       =^c fn^r (self : Ptr self) ...,
                   expand    =^c fn^r (self : Ptr self) ...,
                   deinit    =^c fn^r (self : Ptr self) ...,
                   "[]"      =^c fn^r (self : Ptr self, i : U32)
                                   addr ((self.items) @^r (i)),
                   span      =^c fn^r (self : Ptr self) ...
                 }) {push, pop, count, "[]", span, deinit}

RELIES ON. decl.form.1, bind.field.1, bind.rt.1, bind.ct.1, bind.ctfield.1,
kw.type.rtgroup.1, decl.apply.3, decl.proj.subset.1, vis.projection.1,
op.members.1, abs.bound.1, kw.member.recv.1, kw.self.desugar.1,
ptr.recv.addr.1.

NOTE. bind.field.1 is stated for `-> self` only, and `List` is `-> {a, b}`.
The corpus reads `self.allocator` at unified.z:182, 184 and 190, so S1 must
apply here or the language's most-used type does not compile. B-4.

NOTE. `items`' initialiser reads `allocator` bare and `capacity` bare;
`expand` reads `self.allocator` and `self.capacity`. In the core these are
two different binders -- the `fn^r` parameter and the `rec` member -- with
one identifier each, made equal by S1. That is a mechanical account of one
half of Z-18, and only of the half about parameters.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3  A RECORD

    [[ N :: (r1: T1, ..., rn: Tn) -> self ]]
      =  N =^c fn^r (r1 : T1, ..., rn : Tn) rec { r1 =^r r1, ..., rn =^r rn }

The record is 3.2 with an empty body and a maximal name set. Nothing is
added; the whole content of the declaration is S1.

WORKED EXAMPLE. json.z:77.

    Span :: (lo: u32, hi: u32) -> self

    Span =^c fn^r (lo : U32, hi : U32) rec { lo =^r lo, hi =^r hi }

Also Error (json.z:268), Entry (json.z:136), Health (ecs.z:258), Hit
(ecs.z:413), all of the same shape.

RELIES ON. bind.bodyless.1, bind.field.1, decl.proj.self.1,
kw.type.rtgroup.1.

NOTE ON Z-04. hole.bodiless.1 says a bodiless declaration is a hole;
bind.bodyless.1 says it is a record. The core decides it without an extra
rule, twice over. First, D2 has no binder by construction and `Span ::` has
one, so `Span` is D1 and not D2. Second, and more usefully, a bodiless
declaration is well formed in the core exactly when its projection
restricts: `keep (rec {}) {...}` and `rec {...}` are terms, and a `-> T` or
`-> ()` body with nothing in it has no tail expression to be the result,
and a `-> oneof` body with nothing in it declares no variants. So the core
predicts that `Span :: (lo, hi) -> self` is legal and that
`alloc :: (&self, n: u32) -> ptr` with no body is not -- which is the
distinction hole.bodiless.1's own illustration was reaching for and could
not state. Z-04 is filed; this is a note on it, not a new report.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.4  A FUNCTION

    [[ N :: <C> (R) -> T { s1; ...; sm; E } ]]
      =  N =^c fn^c (C) fn^r (R) <<s1; ...; sm; E>>

where `<<...>>` is the statement layer and is not translated. See B-9.

WORKED EXAMPLE. unified.z:325-329.

    to_string :: <T, M: Allocator, F: Formattable<T>>
                 (allocator: M, v: T) -> slice<u8> {
        b := StringWriter(allocator)
        F.format(v, &b)
        b.finish()
    }

    to_string =^c fn^c (T,
                        M : fit M Allocator,
                        F : fit F (Formattable @^c (T)))
                    fn^r (allocator : M, v : T)
                      <<  b := StringWriter @^c (M) @^r (allocator) ;
                          (F . format) @^r (v, addr b) ;
                          (b . finish) @^r ()  >>

RELIES ON. decl.form.1, decl.proj.value.1, ct.type.1, ct.policy.1,
ct.named.1, abs.bound.1, decl.apply.3.

THE ATTEMPTED REDUCTION, AND WHY IT FAILS. A `-> T` body looks like it
ought to be `keep (rec { ... }) {result}` -- a block projecting exactly one
member. Two things stop it. The member has no name and no member form
declares it: `::`, `:=` and `name : T` are the three member forms and none
of them spells "the value of the block". And the members are ordered:
`b.finish()` must run after `F.format(v, &b)`, while order.independent.1
makes members of a block order-independent. So `-> T` projects something a
block cannot contain, in an order a block does not have. B-9.

NOTE. `b := StringWriter(allocator)` is a `:=` binding inside a `-> T`
body, and bind.which.named.1 says that under `-> SomeType` a member is
either `=` or `::`. Either a function-body local is not a member, in which
case a block has two kinds of contents and is not "the only container", or
it is, in which case bind.which.named.1 is false of every function in the
corpus. B-13.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.5  A PROCEDURE

    [[ n :: (&self, R) -> () { B } ]]
      =  n =^c fn^r (self : Ptr self, R) <<B ; ()>>

This is 3.4 at T = Unit. `-> ()` is not a sixth thing; it is
decl.proj.value.1 with the unit type, and the identity of the unit type and
the unit value (spec.txt, TYPES, The unit type, marked INVENTED) makes the
tail expression write itself.

WORKED EXAMPLE. unified.z:166-170.

    push :: (&self, item: T) -> () {
        if (self.count + 1 > self.capacity) { self.expand() }
        self.items[self.count] = item
        self.count += 1
    }

    push =^c fn^r (self : Ptr self, item : T) << ... ; () >>

RELIES ON. decl.proj.unit.1, decl.proj.value.1, kw.self.recvptr.1,
kw.self.desugar.1, ptr.recv.addr.1, kw.member.recv.1, kw.recv.rule.1.

RESULT. This is the one genuine reduction among the five projections. Five
surface projections, and exactly one of them is another one in disguise.

3.6  A SUM

    [[ N :: <C> -> oneof { n1: T1, ..., nk: Tk } ]]
      =  N =^c fn^c (C) sum { n1 : T1, ..., nk : Tk }

E7, and not E5. The attempt to translate it as E5 is in B-1.

WORKED EXAMPLES. unified.z:21-24, unified.z:26-29, json.z:243-250.

    Option :: <T> -> oneof { none: ()  some: T }

    Option =^c fn^c (T) sum { none : Unit, some : T }

    Result :: <T, E> -> oneof { ok: T  err: E }

    Result =^c fn^c (T, E) sum { ok : T, err : E }

    Json :: <M: Allocator> -> oneof {
        null:   ()
        truth:  bool
        number: f64
        text:   slice<u8>
        array:  List<Json<M>, M>
        object: Map<slice<u8>, Json<M>, M, KY = str_key>
    }

    Json =^c fn^c (M : fit M Allocator)
               sum { null   : Unit,
                     truth  : Bool,
                     number : F64,
                     text   : Slice @^c (U8),
                     array  : List @^c (Json @^c (M), M),
                     object : Map  @^c (Slice @^c (U8), Json @^c (M), M,
                                        KY = str_key) }

RELIES ON. decl.proj.oneof.1, hole.meaning.1, hole.disjunctive.1,
hole.disjunctive.2, hole.oneof.variant.1, hole.oneof.nortgroup.1,
hole.sum.nofield.1, hole.quantify.1, kw.type.oneof.1, ct.named.1.

NOTE. `Json` mentions `Json` under a compile-time application, so E7 must
sit inside a compile-time fixpoint and not only inside E5's. oq.8 asks what
monomorphisation does to a type that contains itself, and this is that
question in core form.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.7  A SHAPE

    [[ N :: <C> -> self { n1: T1, ..., nk: Tk } ]]
      =  N =^c fn^c (C) rec { n1 : T1, ..., nk : Tk }

D2 members only. No new form. This is the cleanest result in the file: a
shape is an ordinary block, all of whose members happen to lack a binder,
and kw.type.hole.1 falls out because a `rec` with a D2 member has no value.

WORKED EXAMPLES. unified.z:81-84, unified.z:125-127, unified.z:37,
ecs.z:457-460.

    Allocator :: -> self {
        alloc: (&self, n: u32) -> ptr
        free:  (&self, p: ptr) -> ()
    }

    Allocator =^c rec {
        alloc : arrow^r ((self : Ptr self, n : U32), Ptr),
        free  : arrow^r ((self : Ptr self, p : Ptr), Unit)
    }

    Fn :: <A, R> -> self { (): (&self, a: A) -> R }

    Fn =^c fn^c (A, R)
             rec { "()" : arrow^r ((self : Ptr self, a : A), R) }

    System :: <M: Allocator> -> self {
        run:  (&self, w: ptr<World<M>>, dt: f32) -> ()
        name: (&self) -> slice<u8>
    }

    System =^c fn^c (M : fit M Allocator)
                 rec { run  : arrow^r ((self : Ptr self,
                                        w : Ptr @^c (World @^c (M)),
                                        dt : F32), Unit),
                       name : arrow^r ((self : Ptr self), Slice @^c (U8)) }

The two uses of a shape:

    [[ <M: Shape> ]]   =  fn^c (M : fit M Shape)      abs.bound.1
    [[ (a: Shape) ]]   =  fn^r (a : Ptr Shape)        abs.erased.1

and the coercion at a use site is E10 again:

    [[ boxed : ptr<Allocator> := &arena ]]
      =  boxed =^r fit (addr arena) (Ptr Allocator)   unified.z:368

RELIES ON. abs.both.1, abs.shape.holesonly.1, abs.shape.1, abs.nomix.1,
hole.def.1, hole.conjunctive.1, kw.type.hole.1, kw.member.recv.1,
abs.bound.1, abs.erased.1, abs.nodyn.1, abs.noadapter.1, mem.borrow.1.

NOTE. E12 (`arrow`) appears here for the first time and is unavoidable: the
hole's type is the type of a function, and the core cannot spell it with
E3, which requires a body. The surface has one production for both --
`FunctionLit = SignatureType [ Block ]` -- and that is exactly the
conflation Z-04 is about, seen from the other end. B-10.

NOTE. Nothing in the core or in the sources forbids a hole with its own
compile-time parameters, e.g. `make: <T> (&self, n: u32) -> ptr<T>`. Such a
shape can be a bound (`fn^c (M : fit M Shape)` monomorphises) but cannot be
erased (`Ptr Shape` is one vtable and a vtable has no room for a family).
No rule states the restriction, and unified.z:86-89 is the corpus routing
around it by hand: the typed layer is put in the module `mem` rather than
in the `Allocator` shape, with a comment explaining the placement in terms
of extensibility rather than in terms of the restriction. B-12.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.8  A POLICY INSTANCE

    [[ N :: <C> -> P { n1 = e1, ..., nj = ej, m1 :: f1, ..., mi :: fi } ]]
      =  N =^c fn^c (C) fit (rec { n1 =^c e1, ..., nj =^c ej,
                                   m1 =^c f1, ..., mi =^c fi })
                             [[ P ]]

Every member becomes D1 at stage c. The two binders `=` and `::` collapse.
See section 5 for what that means.

WORKED EXAMPLES. unified.z:222-228, unified.z:274-282, ecs.z:697-736.

    ord_u8 :: -> Ord<u8> {
        compare = (a, b) -> i32 {
            if (a < b) { return -1 }
            if (b < a) { return  1 }
            return 0
        }
    }

    ord_u8 =^c fit (rec {
                 compare =^c fn^r (a : U8, b : U8) << ... >>
               }) (Ord @^c (U8))

    hex_u8 :: -> Formattable<u8> {
        format = (v, w) -> () {
            digits :: "0123456789abcdef"
            hi := digits[v / 16]
            lo := digits[v % 16]
            w.write_bytes(&hi, 1)
            w.write_bytes(&lo, 1)
        }
    }

    hex_u8 =^c fit (rec {
                 format =^c fn^r (v : U8, w : Ptr Writer) << ... >>
               }) (Formattable @^c (U8))

A parameterised instance with a `::` helper, which is the case that shows
where the fixpoint sits. ecs.z:697-736:

    dump :: <M, indent: u32 = 2> -> fmt.Formattable<World<M>> {
        format = (v, w) -> () { ... self.row(v, e, w) ... }
        row :: (v: World<M>, e: Entity, w: ptr<Writer>) -> ()
            { self.pad(w) ... }
        pad :: (w: ptr<Writer>) -> ()
            { for (i := 0; i < indent; i += 1) { io.write(w, " ") } }
    }

    dump =^c fn^c (M, indent : U32 = 2)
               fit (rec {
                 format =^c fn^r (v : World @^c (M), w : Ptr Writer)
                              << ... (self . row) @^r (v, e, w) ... >>,
                 row    =^c fn^r (v : World @^c (M), e : Entity,
                                  w : Ptr Writer)
                              << (self . pad) @^r (w) ... >>,
                 pad    =^c fn^r (w : Ptr Writer)
                              << ... indent ... >>
               }) (fmt.Formattable @^c (World @^c (M)))

E5's `self` is bound INSIDE `fn^c`, so it carries `M` and `indent`. That is
a mechanical account of ecs.z:689-691: `self.row(...)` is
`(self . row)` where `self` is this application's record, and
`dump.row(...)` is `((dump @^c ()) . row)`, a second application with the
default `indent = 2`. Two spellings, two terms, and the difference is
visible in the core where the surface needs a comment to explain it.

RELIES ON. abs.both.1, abs.policy.1, kw.policy.split.1, bind.which.named.1,
bind.assign.1, kw.value.1, ct.policy.1, ct.value.1, ct.named.1,
ct.named.forced.1, ct.nopool.1, ct.delegate.1, ct.named.decl.1,
decl.apply.4.

NOTE. `compare = (a, b) -> i32 { ... }` gives its parameters no types. The
hole `compare: (a: T, b: T) -> i32` has them. No rule says the fill's
parameter types come from the hole, and oq.14 asks the same question about
the projection and not about the parameters. B-14.

NOTE. `spaced` (json.z:518-556) uses both `self.format` and
`json_fmt<M>.format` in one block, one recursion and one delegation. In the
core they are `(self . format)` and `((json_fmt @^c (M)) . format)`, and
nothing further is needed: ct.delegate.1's "this is ordinary name lookup"
is exactly right and the core confirms it.

3.9  A CLOSURE

    [[ N :: <C> (R) -> self { () :: (&self, A) -> R2 { B } } ]]
      =  N =^c fn^c (C) fn^r (R)
                 rec { R-as-members,                     -- S1
                       "()" =^c fn^r (self : Ptr self, A) <<B>> }

    [[ p(7) ]]  =  (p . "()") @^r (7)

WORKED EXAMPLE. unified.z:382-388.

    print_u8 :: <M: Allocator> (allocator: M) -> self {
        () :: (&self, v: u8) -> () {
            io.write(io.out, to_string<F = fmt_u8>(self.allocator, v))
        }
    }
    p := print_u8(heap)
    p(7)

    print_u8 =^c fn^c (M : fit M Allocator)
                   fn^r (allocator : M)
                     rec {
                       allocator =^r allocator,          -- S1
                       "()" =^c fn^r (self : Ptr self, v : U8)
                                  << (io . write) @^r (io.out,
                                       to_string @^c (F = fmt_u8)
                                                 @^r (self.allocator, v)) >>
                     }

    p =^r print_u8 @^c () @^r (heap)
    (p . "()") @^r (7)

and the erasure two lines later, unified.z:392-393:

    f =^r fit (addr p) (Ptr (Fn @^c (U8, Unit)))
    (f . "()") @^r (8)

RELIES ON. op.closure.1, op.closure.capture.1, op.members.1, op.nocurry.1,
bind.field.1, ptr.deref.spelling.1, ptr.reach.symbolic.1, abs.shape.1,
abs.erased.1.

RESULT. The closure is the strongest confirmation of the claim in the file.
It needs no core form of its own, no capture list, no environment, and no
function type: it is 3.2 with one member whose name happens to be `()`, and
`op.closure.capture.1` is not a rule about closures at all -- it is S1
applied to a block that has a `()` member.

NOTE. `p(7)` and `Heap()` (unified.z:337) are one surface form and two core
terms: `(p . "()") @^r (7)` and `Heap @^r ()`. The discriminator is
kw.type.test.1 applied to the callee -- `Heap` has a runtime group and is a
type, `p` has none and is a value -- so no expression can be elaborated
without first deciding the type-or-value question about its head. B-17.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.10  A VARIANT AS A CONSTRUCTOR FUNCTION

    [[ S<A>.n(e) ]]  =  ((S @^c (A)) # n) @^r (e)          when Tn is not Unit
    [[ S<A>.n()  ]]  =  ((S @^c (A)) # n) @^r ()           when Tn is Unit

and the eliminator, which has no counterpart anywhere else in the language:

    [[ match (v) { n1 (x) { E1 }  n2 { E2 }  _ { E3 } } ]]
      =  case v { n1(x) -> <<E1>>, n2(_) -> <<E2>>, _(_) -> <<E3>> }

WORKED EXAMPLES. unified.z:175, unified.z:177, json.z:167, json.z:392.

    Option<u8>.some(3)     ->  ((Option @^c (U8)) # some) @^r (3)
    Option<T>.none()       ->  ((Option @^c (T))  # none) @^r ()
    Slot.live(Entry(k, v)) ->  (Slot # live) @^r (Entry @^r (k, v))
    Json.array(xs)         ->  (Json # array) @^r (xs)

RELIES ON. hole.ctor.1, hole.oneof.variant.1, hole.disjunctive.1,
match.arm.1, match.exhaustive.1, match.expr.1, ct.expected.1,
ct.typecarries.1.

NOTE. E8 is spelled `#` and E2 is spelled `.`, and the surface spells both
`.`. `Option<u8>.some` reaches a hole through a type, which
ct.typenotvalue.1 forbids in the same words it forbids `Ord<u8>.compare`.
B-3.

NOTE. hole.ctor.1 says the derived function is `(T) -> Self`. For
`none: ()` that is `(()) -> Option<T>` and the call is `none(())`. The
corpus writes `none()` at thirty-two sites. B-2.


============================================================================
4  WHAT DOES NOT REDUCE
============================================================================

Eighteen entries. Each is one line, then the detail. Entries marked FILED
are already in issues.txt or open.txt and are cross-referenced rather than
re-reported; the rest appear to be new, and each says what would have to be
written for the translation to close.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
B-1  `-> oneof` needs three core forms of its own and shares only the
     header and the member spelling with `-> self`.

This is the central pressure point and the translation gives a clean
answer. The attempted reduction is this: give E5 a fill discipline K in
{all, one}, so that `rec_all` requires every D2 member to be filled and
`rec_one` requires exactly one. That much works, and it is a real
observation: the INTRODUCTION forms of `-> self` and `-> oneof` genuinely
are one thing read with two cardinalities.

Everything after introduction diverges.

    ELIMINATION. Under K = all, E2 is total: `v.alloc` always denotes.
    Under K = one, E2 is partial: `v.some` denotes only if `some` was the
    hole that got filled. Nothing in E1-E6 makes partiality observable, so
    the core cannot recover the information. E9 is required, and E9 has no
    counterpart under `-> self`: there is no `case` over a struct and no
    member selection on a sum.

    REPRESENTATION. E9 needs a tag, and the tag is a value that no member
    form declares. spec.txt says so itself under MATCH ("A sum's
    discriminant is not a value"), and the corpus invents `tag_of` to reach
    it (json.z:381-387, filed as Z-39) and writes a five-arm match to ask a
    one-bit question (ecs.z:272-273). E7 is required.

    DERIVATION. Under K = one, every hole derives a function (hole.ctor.1).
    Under K = all, no hole derives anything and the spelling that would
    reach one is explicitly forbidden (ct.typenotvalue.1). E8 is required.

    ABSTRACTNESS. kw.type.hole.1 says a block with an unfilled hole is a
    type. hole.disjunctive.2 says a `-> oneof` block is never abstract.
    Both are true of `Option<T>`, which has two unfilled holes. So a hole
    set is an OBLIGATION under K = all and a SIGNATURE under K = one, and
    one representation cannot be both.

    STAGE. hole.oneof.nortgroup.1 deletes the runtime group and
    hole.sum.nofield.1 deletes the `:=` member, so `-> oneof` has no
    runtime stage at all. `fn^r` never appears in 3.6 and appears in every
    other translation in section 3.

VERDICT ON THE PRESSURE POINT. `-> oneof` is not `-> self` with holes read
disjunctively. It is a second construct that shares a header. The one thing
the two do share -- the introduction form -- is real and is worth keeping;
the design should say that and stop there.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
B-2  hole.ctor.1's derived function has the wrong arity for every unit
     variant, at thirty-two sites in the corpus.

hole.ctor.1: "A variant name is also a derived function `(T) -> Self`." For
`none: ()`, T is the unit type, so the derived function is
`(()) -> Option<T>` and the call is `Option<T>.none(())`. The grammar
supports it: `UnitValue = "(" ")"` is an Operand and `Arguments` takes an
ExpressionList, so `none(())` is one argument and `none()` is zero.

The corpus writes `none()`. Thirty-two sites: unified.z:175, json.z:174,
177, 178, 185, 291, 294-299, 304, 363, 377, 392, 433, ecs.z:166, 431, 597,
605, 610, 617, 618, 637, 638, 643, 644, 790, and the two commentary
occurrences at json.z:827 and ecs.z:945, 1145.

WHAT WOULD CLOSE IT. One sentence: the derived function of a variant whose
payload type is `()` takes no arguments. It is a special case and it should
be written down as one, because "a variant name is also a derived function
`(T) -> Self`" reads like a uniform rule and is not one.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
B-3  ct.typenotvalue.1 forbids the spelling hole.ctor.1 requires.

ct.typenotvalue.1: "A type is never a value. A hole cannot be reached
through a type: `Ord<u8>.compare` is not a spelling." `Option<u8>` is a
type by kw.type.oneof.1 and `some` is one of its holes by
hole.oneof.variant.1, so `Option<u8>.some` is a hole reached through a
type, in exactly the form the rule names.

The intended reconciliation is presumably that the derived function is a
separate member that happens to share the hole's name, so `Option<u8>.some`
selects the derivation rather than the hole. The core makes the cost of
that reading explicit: two members named `some` live on one block, one of
type `u8` and one of type `(u8) -> Option<u8>`, and E2 must consult the
fill discipline to know which it returns. Nothing states this.

WHAT WOULD CLOSE IT. Scope ct.typenotvalue.1 to conjunctive hole sets, or
say that the derived constructor is a member and that it shadows the hole.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
B-4  bind.field.1 is stated for `-> self` and the corpus needs it under
     `-> {a, b}`.

bind.field.1: "A runtime parameter of a `-> self` declaration is a field of
the resulting block." `List` (unified.z:157) projects
`-> {push, pop, count, [], span, deinit}`, not `-> self`, and its methods
read `self.allocator` at unified.z:182, 184 and 190. So S1 must apply to
restricting projections generally.

Confirmed by every other struct in the corpus with a partial projection,
and by `probe` (json.z:151), which is the one place S1 must NOT apply:
`probe` has a runtime parameter `k` and a projection `{at, found}`, and `k`
is not a member of anything.

WHAT WOULD CLOSE IT. Restate bind.field.1 for restricting projections, and
say that a projection with typed members is not one of them. That second
half depends on Z-19.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
B-5  S1 is a side condition the surface syntax does not express, and it has
     three exceptions the surface does not mark.

S1 turns a binder into a member. It is the whole content of the record
declaration (3.3) and half the content of the struct (3.2), and it is not
visible anywhere in `Name <args> (args) -> proj { body }`: the parameter
list is written to the LEFT of the projection that decides whether its
entries are parameters or fields. A translator must read to the arrow
before it knows what the parameter list meant.

Its exceptions: the receiver is never injected (S2); a `-> T` or `-> ()`
declaration injects nothing; and a `-> {a: T, b: U}` declaration injects
nothing either, though it is written with the same production as one that
does.

WHAT WOULD CLOSE IT. Nothing, short of a syntax change. This is a genuine
cost of putting the projection after the parameters, and it should be
recorded as one rather than as a property of `-> self`.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
B-6  `-> T { body }` has two meanings and no rule names the discriminator.

    to_string :: <...> (allocator: M, v: T) -> slice<u8> { ...; b.finish() }
    ord_u8    :: -> Ord<u8> { compare = (a, b) -> i32 { ... } }

Both are decl.proj.value.1. The first body's tail expression is the result.
The second body has no tail expression; it is a set of fills for `Ord<u8>`'s
holes. `[[ ... ]]` for the first ends in `<<...>>` and for the second in
`fit (rec {...}) [[T]]`. Two translations, one surface form.

Three candidate discriminators, all of which the corpus tests:

    (a) "T has holes". Fails: `parse :: <M: Allocator> (allocator, src)
        -> Result<Json<M>, Error>` (json.z:453) projects a type with two
        holes and is an ordinary function.

    (b) "there is no runtime group". Holds across the whole corpus, and is
        never stated. It also gives no answer for `x :: -> u32 { 3 }`,
        which no rule forbids and which kw.value.1 says is a value.

    (c) "T's holes are conjunctive, i.e. T is a `-> self` block with
        holes". Holds across the corpus, and consults three properties of
        a type that is written in a projection position where the reader is
        expecting a result type.

bind.which.named.1 is the nearest rule and describes only case (c); see
B-13 for what it does to case (a).

WHAT WOULD CLOSE IT. Name the discriminator. (c) is the right one and it
means saying, in the section on projections, that `-> T` where T is a
conjunctive hole set is a different act from `-> T` where it is not.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
B-7  hole.quantify.1 and vis.projection.1 contradict each other on `List`.

hole.quantify.1: "The projection quantifies over holes only. `::` members
are unaffected, so a `-> oneof` block carries methods exactly as a
`-> self` block does."

vis.projection.1: "The projection is the export list. `-> self` exports the
whole block; `-> {a, b}` exports those two names."

`expand` (unified.z:180) is a `::` member of `List` and is not in `List`'s
projection. Under hole.quantify.1 it is unaffected by the projection and
escapes. Under vis.projection.1 it does not. The whole point of `List`'s
closed projection, argued at unified.z:151-156, is that `items`, `capacity`
and by implication `expand` do not escape.

In the core the two rules are two different operations on one term: E6
restricts a name set without regard to how the names were bound, which is
vis.projection.1; hole.quantify.1 asks E6 to skip D1 members, which would
make E6 a no-op for every block whose members are all bound.

The charitable reading is that hole.quantify.1 is about `-> oneof` only and
its first sentence overreaches. The core cannot take the charitable reading
because it has one restriction operator.

WHAT WOULD CLOSE IT. Scope hole.quantify.1 to `-> oneof`, where it is doing
real work, and delete its claim about the projection in general.

Note also that hole.quantify.1's payoff -- a sum carrying methods -- is
used at zero sites in the corpus. Every helper over a sum is a free
function: `unwrap_or` and `is_some` (json.z:57-66), `is_dead` (ecs.z:272).

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
B-8  ProjectionList is restriction in one place and construction in the
     other, and the two are opposite operations. FILED: Z-19.

Recorded here only because the core sharpens it. `-> {push, pop, ...}`
(unified.z:157) is E6: it names members that exist and removes the others,
constructs nothing, and leaves the block's identity and lifetime alone.
`-> {at: u32, found: bool}` (json.z:151) is the reverse: it names members
that do not exist in the block, and json.z:156-159 constructs a fresh
anonymous record at each `return`.

One production, and in the core one of them shrinks a record while the
other builds one. Z-19 files this as a gap in the rules; the core's reading
is that they are not one form with two readings but two forms with one
spelling, and that `-> {a, b}` should be split before it is specified.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
B-9  `-> T` and `-> ()` project an unnamed, ordered result that no member
     form declares. This is the boundary with the statement layer.

Set out in 3.4. Two of the five projections cannot be given meaning in the
declaration layer at all: they name the value of a body, and the value of a
body is the value of its last statement, which spec.txt marks INVENTED and
which Z-01 says is unspecified along with everything else in that layer.

The consequence for the claim is worth stating plainly: of the five
projections offered as five readings of one construct, two are not
declaration-layer constructs. They are the function's result type, and
their meaning is owed by a layer that does not exist yet.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
B-10 A declaration and the type of a declaration are one surface
     production, and the core needs two forms. ROOT OF: Z-04.

`FunctionLit = SignatureType [ Block ]`. The body is optional, and dropping
it turns a declaration into its own type. The core cannot do that: E3 needs
a body and E12 is a separate former. Every hole of every shape in the
corpus needs E12 -- `Allocator.alloc`, `Writer.write_bytes`,
`Fn."()"`, `System.run`, `Ord.compare`, `Key.hash`, `Formattable.format`,
`AnyStore.remove` -- and no surface form distinguishes them from a
declaration someone forgot to finish.

Z-04 files the contradiction between hole.bodiless.1 and bind.bodyless.1.
This entry files its cause: the two rules are trying to decide a question
the grammar refused to ask, and 3.3 shows a clean answer falls out of the
core (a bodiless declaration is well formed exactly when its projection
restricts).

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
B-11 `<T>` is a hole whose filler is a type, which hole.valueonly.1 and
     hole.noassoc.1 deny.

The pressure point asks this about `Formattable<T>` and `Key<K>`, and those
two are fine: `format: (v: T, w: ptr<Writer>) -> ()` and
`hash: (k: K) -> u64` are holes of VALUES whose types mention a type
parameter, which is exactly hole.valueonly.1 plus hole.typeparam.1 working
as designed. The break is one level out, in the parameter list itself.

    TypeParam = identifier [ ":" Type ] [ "=" Expression ]

That is the same shape as a member: an identifier, an optional ascription,
an optional filler. Read against bind.hole.1, `<T>` is an identifier with
no ascription and no binder, which is a hole. Its filler is `u8`. So the
compile-time parameter list contains holes of kind TYPE, and
hole.noassoc.1 says "There is no hole of type `type`, and therefore no
associated type", and hole.valueonly.1 says "Every hole is a hole of a
value".

The design escapes by calling `<T>` a parameter rather than a hole. The
core cannot: bind.field.1 has already made a parameter a member, and D2 has
already made an unbound member a hole, so the two categories are the same
category and the kind distinction has to be real. In the core, E3 at stage
c binds variables of two kinds -- types and values -- and E4 at stage c
applies both, and ct.kinds.1 says as much ("a type, a policy or a value")
three sections away from the two rules that deny it.

The compile-time parameter list contains all three kinds and spells them
almost identically:

    <T>                     a hole of kind type
    <F: Formattable<T>>     a hole of kind value, ascribed
    <indent: u32 = 2>       a binding of kind value, with a default
    <M: Allocator>          a hole of kind value whose ascription is a
                            conjunctive hole set, i.e. a bound

WHAT WOULD CLOSE IT. Say that hole.valueonly.1 and hole.noassoc.1 are about
MEMBER holes, and that a compile-time parameter may have kind type. That is
a small edit and it makes ct.kinds.1 consistent with the two rules it
currently contradicts.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
B-12 A hole may carry compile-time parameters, and then its shape can be a
     bound but not erased. No rule says so; the corpus routes around it.

Set out in the second note to 3.7. `abs.bound.1` monomorphises, so a
generic hole is fine behind `<M: Shape>`. `abs.erased.1` makes
`(a: Shape)` a `ptr<Shape>` with a vtable, and a vtable has one slot per
member, not one per instantiation. So a shape with a generic hole is
usable one way and not the other, and abs.nodyn.1's "the bracket separates
static from dynamic" quietly stops being true for it.

unified.z:86-89 is the corpus avoiding this without naming it: `mem.make`
is generic and lives in a module of free functions rather than in the
`Allocator` shape, and the comment explains the placement in terms of
extensibility.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
B-13 bind.which.named.1 admits only `=` and `::` under `-> T`, and every
     function body in the corpus writes `:=`.

bind.which.named.1: "Under `-> SomeType`, a member matching one of that
type's holes is written `=`. Any other member is fresh and is written
`::`." `to_string` is `-> slice<u8>` and writes `b := StringWriter(...)`
(unified.z:326). `probe` writes `i :=` and `free :=` (json.z:152-153).
`parse` writes `lx :=`, `p :=`, `v :=`, `t :=` (json.z:454-457).

The rule survives only if a binding introduced inside a body is not a
member of the block. But BLOCKS AND SCOPE says a block is the only
container, `Statement = Declaration | ...` makes it a declaration, and
order.independent.1 makes declarations in a block order-independent, which
the corpus visibly relies on being false inside a body.

So either a block has two kinds of contents -- members and locals -- and
"every declaration builds a block of named values" is not the whole story,
or bind.which.named.1 is false in every function. The core needs the
distinction either way: `rec` is unordered and recursive, `<<...>>` is
ordered and sequential, and the surface uses one pair of braces for both.

WHAT WOULD CLOSE IT. Say whether a body-local `:=` is a member. This is a
declaration-layer question with a declaration-layer answer, and it does not
wait on Z-01.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
B-14 The parameters of a lambda filling a hole are given no types and no
     rule supplies them.

`compare = (a, b) -> i32 { ... }` (unified.z:223) against the hole
`compare: (a: T, b: T) -> i32` (unified.z:212). Same at unified.z:234, 267,
275, 289, 300, json.z:103, 110, 123, 124, 468, 519, ecs.z:128, 129, 667,
699.

The grammar admits it -- `Parameter = Receiver | identifier [ ":" Type ]
[ "=" Expression ]` -- and nothing states that the omitted types come from
the hole being filled. oq.14 asks the same question about the PROJECTION
("whether a lambda filling a typed hole may elide its projection, since the
hole already supplies it") and the corpus never elides the projection and
always elides the parameter types, so oq.14 is asking about the case that
does not arise and not about the one that does.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
B-15 `::` and `:=` are a staging discipline the core needs, and
     order.independent.1 denies the ordering the corpus relies on.

E5 is a recursive unordered record. The corpus needs it to be two ordered
phases. `Map` (json.z:132-146) declares `Entry` (136) and `Slot` (138) as
`::` members and then initialises the `:=` field `slots` (146) by passing
`Slot` as a
compile-time argument, so the compile-time members must be resolved before
the runtime fields are. `List` (unified.z:158-164) initialises `items` from
`capacity`, and json.z:350 asserts "fields initialise in declaration
order", which no rule states and which order.independent.1 appears to deny.

The core can express the staging -- `=^c` before `=^r` -- and cannot
express the ordering within a stage without the statement layer. That half
belongs to Z-01 and is not pursued here. The half that does not: nothing
says that `::` members are resolved before `:=` fields, and every container
in the corpus depends on it.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
B-16 Structural fit is a primitive, and the shape/policy classification
     does not constrain which side of the fit is written.

E10 is not derivable from E1-E6. Membership (`ord_u8 : Ord<u8>`, written at
the declaration) and subsumption (`Heap` satisfies `Allocator`, checked at
the use site) are one operation in the core, which is a real reduction and
the answer to the fourth pressure point: THE CORE DOES NOT NEED TO KNOW
`::` FROM `=`. A member is written `=` iff its name is a hole of the
projected type, so the binder is a redundancy check and carries no
information the core cannot recover.

What the core then shows is that the classification is one-directional.
abs.shape.1 and abs.policy.1 classify a HOLE SET by whether its holes take
a receiver. They do not constrain how an instance is written, and only one
of the two directions is closed:

    A SHAPE CANNOT BE FILLED NOMINALLY, and this does fall out.
    `x :: -> Allocator { alloc = ... }` makes `x` a value by kw.value.1;
    kw.recv.rule.1 says a receiver is required exactly when the block is a
    type; the hole takes `&self`; so there is nothing for the receiver to
    receive. Good -- no rule needed.

    A POLICY CAN BE SATISFIED STRUCTURALLY, and nothing says whether that
    is intended. `Ord<u8>`'s hole takes no receiver, so any value block
    with a matching `compare` satisfies it, and `sort<O = anything>` will
    take it. `saturating` (unified.z:70-72) is exactly this: a value block
    of receiver-free `::` members, structurally a policy instance of a
    policy nobody declared, used as a module. `mem` (unified.z:90-95) has
    the same shape and is a module too.

So the binder difference makes the shape case visible and the policy case
invisible, and U.9.4 ("a block satisfies a shape by accident") is a
statement about half of the relation.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
B-17 `p(7)` and `Heap()` are one surface form and two core operations.

Set out in the note to 3.9. `Heap()` is E4 at stage r. `p(7)` is E2 then
E4. `decl.apply.4` decides between them, and deciding requires knowing
whether the head is a declaration with an unapplied runtime group or a
value with a `()` member -- that is, kw.type.test.1 must be run on the head
of every call before the call can be elaborated. It is resolvable and it is
not free, and ptr.deref.spelling.1's long form `f.()(x)`, which would make
it explicit, is used at zero sites in the corpus and is flagged in spec.txt
as possibly only a gloss.

The case that has no answer at all: a declaration with both a runtime group
and a `()` member. `B(x)` applies the group by decl.apply.4 and `B(x)(y)`
invokes the member, but the corpus never writes one and no rule addresses
it.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
B-18 `rec` must be a fixpoint at both stages, and the compile-time one is
     an open question. FILED: oq.8.

E5 binds `self` and every member name in every member, which is what
`fmt_u8.format` calling `fmt_u8` (unified.z:268) and `self.format` inside
`spaced` (json.z:526) need. That much is ordinary.

`Json` (json.z:243-250) mentions `Json<M>` inside its own variant types, so
E7 must sit inside a compile-time fixpoint as well: `Json` is not finished
when `array: List<Json<M>, M>` is elaborated. oq.8 asks what
monomorphisation does to a type that contains itself, and this is the same
question with the recursion made syntactic. Cross-referenced, not
re-reported.


============================================================================
5  CONSEQUENCES
============================================================================

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
What the core makes obvious that the surface hides

`-> self` IS NOT A MARKER. It is maximal restriction: `-> self` and
`-> {a, b}` are one operation, E6, differing only in the size of the name
set. rules.txt's own table calls `-> self` "struct / module / shape" and
`-> {a, b}` "multiple return, AND visibility", which reads as two entries
and is one. What actually makes a block a struct is having a runtime group
(kw.type.rtgroup.1), and `List` proves it: it is the corpus's central
struct and its projection is `-> {a, b}`.

THE MODULE IS NOT A CONSTRUCT AT ALL. `io`, `mem`, `saturating` and
`ord_u8` translate to the same core term modulo the projection: a rec of
compile-time members, no runtime group, no holes. Module, namespace,
arithmetic helper and policy instance are one thing with four names. This
is the claim's strongest result and it holds without qualification.

THE CLOSURE IS NOT A CONSTRUCT EITHER. 3.9 needs nothing that 3.2 does not
already need. `op.closure.capture.1` is S1 with a different name.

THE BINDER IS REDUNDANT. `=` versus `::` under a named projection carries
no information the core cannot recover from the projected type's hole set.
It is a check, not a distinction. That is a good property and the design
should claim it more strongly than it does.

`self` VERSUS THE NAME IS MECHANICAL. `self` is E5's fixpoint variable,
bound inside `fn^c`; the bare name is the same declaration applied to its
defaults. That is why `dump.row(...)` resets `indent` to 2 and
`self.row(...)` does not (ecs.z:689-691), and why `json_fmt<M>.format` and
`self.format` mean opposite things in one block (json.z:518-556). The
corpus explains this twice in prose; the core needs no explanation.

VISIBILITY IS FREE. vis.opaque.1 -- a value escaping while its type does
not -- is not a rule about types. It is E6 restricting a name set and not
touching what the names hold. `io.out` needs no machinery.

BODILESSNESS IS DECIDABLE. A bodiless declaration is well formed exactly
when its projection restricts. Neither reading in Z-04 says this and it
falls out of the core in one line.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Where the core shows two things the design calls the same are not

THE PROJECTION IS NOT ONE THING. Five surface projections, four core
operations, and exactly one genuine reduction:

    -> ()          E3 body, tail = unit     REDUCES to -> T
    -> T           E3 body, tail typed      result type
    -> self        E6, maximal              restriction
    -> {a, b}      E6, partial              restriction
    -> {a: T}      fresh record             CONSTRUCTION -- opposite of E6
    -> oneof       E7                       a different construct

So the three uses named in the brief -- visibility, multiple return, the
struct/module marker -- do NOT elaborate the same way. Visibility and the
struct marker are the same operation (E6). Multiple return is its
opposite. And the result type is a fourth thing that is not in the
declaration layer at all.

`-> oneof` IS A SECOND CONSTRUCT. B-1. The introduction forms coincide; the
elimination, the representation, the derivation and the stage discipline
all diverge. Three of the fourteen core forms exist for it alone.

A HOLE IS TWO THINGS. Under a conjunctive reading it is an obligation on
whoever instantiates, whose filler has the hole's own type, and which
cannot be reached through the type. Under a disjunctive reading it is a
labelled injection, whose derived filler has type `(T) -> Self`, and which
is reached through the type at every construction site in the corpus. The
design says "a hole means a requirement under `-> self` and a variant under
`-> oneof`, and that is the whole of both interfaces and sums". The first
half is exactly right and the interface case reduces perfectly (3.7). The
second half is a second construct wearing the first one's syntax.

A PARAMETER IS TWO THINGS. Under a restricting projection it is a field
(S1); otherwise it is a binder. The projection that decides is written
after it.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The verdict

IT IS NOT ONE CONSTRUCT. It is TWO, plus one whose status cannot be
decided until the statement layer exists.

    ONE.    A block: a recursive record whose members are bound at compile
            time, bound at run time, or unbound. This single form absorbs
            module, struct, record, shape, policy type, policy instance,
            closure, namespace, visibility, bounds and erasure -- six of
            the eight things the claim names, plus several it does not.
            Ten core forms. The claim is TRUE here, and more strongly than
            the design argues it: the shape is not a block with a special
            property, it is a block whose members happen to have no
            binders, and the closure is not a construct at all.

    TWO.    A tagged block. Shares the header and the member spelling with
            the block and nothing below them. Three core forms of its own,
            its own eliminator, its own representation, its own derived
            functions, and its own stage discipline. The claim is FALSE
            here.

    PENDING. A function. `-> T` and `-> ()` project a value that no member
            form declares, in an order a block does not have. It reduces to
            the block if and only if the statement layer supplies an
            ordered anonymous result member. That layer is Z-01 and is
            unwritten, so the question is open rather than answered either
            way. It is worth noting which way it is likely to fall: a
            function body is ordered and a block is not, so the honest
            expectation is a third construct rather than a reduction.

The claim as stated -- "module, struct, function, procedure, sum type,
interface, closure and policy are ALL ONE CONSTRUCT read differently" -- is
therefore right about six of its eight terms, wrong about the seventh
(sum), and undecided about the eighth (function, with procedure as its unit
case). That is a strong result for a language design and a weak one for the
sentence, and the difference between the two is worth the edit: the
sentence should name the block, say that a shape and a policy and a closure
and a module are all it, and stop before the sum.
